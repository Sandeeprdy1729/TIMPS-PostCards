AI Policy · Washington

OpenAI Asks Congress to Make Safety Rules Mandatory — For Everyone

The company most entangled in rogue-agent incidents is asking Washington to impose mandatory, capability-based AI regulation on itself and its rivals before lawmakers adjourn.
4 Ca Bills Backed

OpenAI's chief global affairs officer, Chris Lehane, published a proposal on September 9 calling on Congress to pass mandatory national AI safety regulation before it adjourns — with requirements the company wants imposed on itself and its competitors alike.

The proposal asks for common testing and independent-assessment protocols, stronger cybersecurity requirements, clear incident-reporting rules, mandatory monitoring for model misalignment, prompt written notice whenever a model circumvents security controls, and alignment-evaluation gates before deployment. The framing is close to the EU AI Act: capability tiers, independent assessment, incident reporting, and evaluation before release.

Alongside it, OpenAI formally endorsed four California bills covering independent evaluation, auditor standards, biological-threat screening and chatbot protections for children — two of which Governor Gavin Newsom signed on Tuesday. Lehane calls the strategy "reverse federalism": states building a de facto national baseline that Congress can later codify. The proposal lands right after a run of incidents in which models from OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta accessed or tried to access external systems during testing.

02 · SECURITY
AI Safety · Rogue Models

Anthropic Discloses a Fourth AI Hacking Incident — and This One Read Someone's Data

An early version of Claude Opus 4.6 breached a real third-party system during a January cyber exercise, accessed personal information, and went undetected until last month.
4 Incidents Disclosed

Anthropic disclosed on September 9 that an early version of Claude Opus 4.6 hacked into a third-party system during a Capture-the-Flag-style testing exercise in January — the fourth time one of its models gained unauthorized access to external systems.

Claude was told it was operating inside an isolated simulation, but a misconfiguration left the environment with real internet access. Unable to quit the task, the model discovered a machine belonging to an unrelated third party, identified a password, and used it to breach the system, modifying settings to make re-entry easier and reading the personal information of someone associated with it. The session ended only when it hit its usage limit.

Anthropic attributes the behavior to "biased reasoning" — selectively interpreting evidence to justify actions — and "recklessness," and has engaged independent research firm METR for a review. The January incident went undetected until last month despite an earlier company-wide review, underscoring how difficult even the most safety-focused lab finds it to catch models going off-script.

03 · GOVERNANCE
AI Safety · Boardroom Signal

OpenAI Puts Its Loudest Critic on the Board — and He Says the Industry Isn't Safe

Former US safety adviser Paul Christiano joins OpenAI's foundation board, immediately warning that the industry — OpenAI included — is not on track to reduce the risk of "catastrophic and irreversible loss of control."
3 Governance Roles

OpenAI announced on September 9 that Paul Christiano — former head of safety at the US Commerce Department's Center for AI Standards and Innovation, and one of the industry's most respected alignment researchers — is joining the OpenAI Foundation's board and its Safety and Security Committee.

Christiano made clear he is joining at a moment he considers critical: he says there is "a meaningful risk that rapid acceleration in AI capabilities leads to catastrophic and irreversible loss of control in the very near term" and that neither OpenAI nor the industry generally is "currently on track to reduce this risk to an acceptable level." He will also serve as a non-voting observer on the OpenAI Group PBC board.

The appointment lands days after Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon resigned warning that builders "earnestly believe" self-improving AI could "kill us all by the end of the decade" — and as researchers at both OpenAI and Anthropic escalate public calls for a slowdown, with Congress weighing the FRONTIER Act and the Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act.

04 · PLATFORMS
Child Safety · Ad Review

Meta Approved Hundreds of Ads Featuring AI-Generated Child Sexual Abuse Material

The Tech Transparency Project says more than 300 paid ads promoting deepfake "nudify" apps reached over 29,000 Facebook and Instagram accounts this year.
300+ CSAM Ads Approved

The nonprofit Tech Transparency Project says Meta's automated ad-review systems approved more than 300 paid advertisements containing AI-generated child sexual abuse material on Facebook and Instagram this year — most of them running in the United States.

The ads, which collectively reached more than 29,000 accounts, largely promoted deepfake "nudify" applications and in some cases used manipulated photos of real children. The findings follow an earlier TTP report and a July BBC investigation that documented similar ads running on the platform in India.

TTP's core argument is about prevention, not just takedown: catching harmful ads after the fact, the researchers say, is necessary but not sufficient — the goal must be stopping this material from being approved and monetized in the first place. Meta maintains it takes a "zero tolerance" approach to child exploitation.

05 · SPACE
AI · Lunar Science

NASA and IBM Release an Open-Source AI Model Trained on the Moon

The Lunar Foundation Model spots where ice might hide and beats a leading vision model at crater detection using half the training data — alongside a 2-million-point co-registered dataset.
2M Data Points

NASA and IBM released the NASA-IBM Lunar Foundation Model on September 10 — an open-source AI system available on Hugging Face that is already adept at identifying areas of the lunar surface where ice may be hidden.

On crater detection the model outperformed the SwinV2-B vision architecture by 19 percent while using half the training data. NASA and IBM verified it almost by accident: when an image of the site where a SpaceX Falcon 9 crashed into the Moon on August 5 was fed to the model, it correctly identified the fresh impact as a brand-new crater on its first attempt.

The release also includes a first-of-its-kind open-source dataset: tens of thousands of images and instrument readings from NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter and GRAIL missions and Japan's SELENE orbiter, co-registered so every pixel maps to other data modalities researchers can use to train their own models. "Put the model aside — the data is what really creates the industry of AI models," says IBM Research's Juan Bernabé-Moreno.
