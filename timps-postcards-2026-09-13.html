White House · Frontier Oversight

Trump Has "No Concerns" About AI Extinction — Losing to China Is His Only Fear

Departing Dallas, the president brushed off extinction warnings from frontier-lab insiders — and told reporters the only thing that worries him is falling behind Beijing.
1 yr Claimed Lead Over China

Asked on the tarmac in Dallas whether he had any concerns about AI ending human civilization, President Donald Trump answered with three words: "No, I don't have any."

"I have concerns that if we don't win AI, we're going to be put in a very bad position," he told reporters Thursday, adding that the United States leads China "by a pretty good period, I would say a year, which is, you know, considered a lot." The dismissal landed at the peak of a feverish week: Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon resigned accusing the labs of "racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives," alignment lead Evan Hubinger put the odds of AI erasing humanity at above 10% within a decade, and Geoffrey Hinton called the estimate "not unreasonable."

The split is now structural. More than a dozen insiders at OpenAI and Anthropic publicly called for a slowdown this week, and a bipartisan group of senators is drafting frontier-oversight legislation — while the White House counters that the race itself is the policy. For anyone reading the frontier through Washington, the signal is unambiguous: capability wins are the only metric the executive branch is scoring.

02 · SAFETY
Antitrust Law · Coordinated Pacing

OpenAI Asks Congress Whether a Coordinated AI Slowdown Would Break Antitrust Law

Worried that labs slowing down together could run afoul of the Sherman Act, OpenAI is testing the legal ground — even as its own chief scientist argues a shared pause is vital.
12+ Insiders Urging Slower Pace

OpenAI has asked members of Congress whether a coordinated industry-wide slowdown in AI development would be legal — wary that labs cooperating on safety could violate US antitrust law under the Sherman Act.

According to WIRED's reporting, Sam Altman told employees at an internal all-hands this week that OpenAI could slow its pace — possibly alongside other labs — though some rivals probably wouldn't go along. Chief scientist Jakub Pachocki has gone further, calling for "mandated safety bars" enforced by "a network of third-party auditors, by government agencies or by international bodies," and hoping "for voluntary slowdowns to become commonplace until shared safety bars are established."

The legal wrinkle is genuinely new. More than 1,000 AI employees petitioned Washington in July for a slowdown mechanism, and a bipartisan bill — the Collaboration on Adversarial Threats and Security Risks Act, which would let labs cooperate on safety — is still parked in the Senate Judiciary Committee. Whether coordinated safety is a conspiracy is now the industry's strangest open question: the frontier labs have spent years competing, and suddenly safety coordination may be the one thing they aren't allowed to do together.

03 · GEOPOLITICS
Model Diplomacy · Project Glasswing

EU Finally Gets Its Hands on Anthropic's Mythos — Just Not the Newest One

After three months of negotiation and a two-week White House access ban, ENISA is now testing Mythos 5. Everyone outside select US organizations is still locked out of 5.1.
3 Frontier Cyber AIs Now with ENISA

The EU's cybersecurity agency ENISA has finally been granted access to Mythos 5 — Anthropic's tightly restricted model for finding and exploiting software vulnerabilities — and is testing it now.

"Following our constructive engagement with Anthropic, we can confirm that the EU's cybersecurity agency ENISA has been granted access to Mythos 5 and is testing it now," European Commission technology-sovereignty spokesperson Thomas Regnier said — more than three months after Anthropic first proposed the access following EU lobbying. The road was rocky: negotiations stalled for months over the scope of access, then the White House abruptly restricted foreign-national access to Mythos 5 and Fable 5 for roughly two weeks this summer. When the directive was lifted on June 30, Fable 5 made a broad comeback while Mythos 5 stayed confined to vetted US organizations.

The victory is only partial. ENISA will not get Mythos 5.1, and the UK's AI Safety Institute — among the first non-US bodies to stress-test the original — was shut out of the new version too. What ENISA now holds is a window onto two frontiers at once: the agency has also received OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Cyber and GPT-6 Astra, giving Europe its first independent view of the very models its officials fear most.

04 · MARKETS
IPO Calendar · Credit Lines

Anthropic Pushes Its Landmark IPO Toward Mid-October — Days Before US Midterms

The prospectus slips to late September and marketing to mid-October as Anthropic finalizes a $15 billion credit line. Investors still whisper of a potential $2 trillion listing.
$15B Revolving Credit Facility

Anthropic is expected to begin marketing its IPO in mid-October at the earliest, with the listing landing days before the November 3 US midterm elections, people familiar with the matter told Reuters.

The prospectus — expected "as early as next week" — has now slipped to late September, the people said, cautioning that plans could still change. As part of the process, Anthropic is finalizing a $15 billion revolving credit facility, six times the $2.5 billion it had a year ago, after which underwriters' analysts are expected to meet with management. Morgan Stanley leads a syndicate that runs seventeen banks deep, with Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Citi in the top roles — and roughly $1.25 billion of committed balance sheet per lead bank is reportedly the clearing price for underwriting position.

Investors still frame this as a potential $2 trillion listing, a test of public-market appetite right after SpaceX's record June debut at $1.77 trillion, which raised $86 billion. Anthropic, which has reportedly aimed to raise around $100 billion, declined to comment. The shifting calendar, analysts note, is as much about market timing as preparation: the roadshow now runs through the most politically charged window of the year.

05 · BUSINESS
China · Open-Weight Growth

Moonshot AI Targets $2 Billion in Annualized Revenue — Double Its August Run Rate

The Kimi maker is telling investors it will hit $2B by year-end, riding the open-weight wave of Kimi K3 as it courts the US cloud giants.
$2B Annualized Revenue Target

Chinese AI star Moonshot AI is targeting $2 billion in annualized revenue by the end of 2026 — double the run rate it reported for August, Bloomberg reported Friday.

The lab, founded by Tsinghua graduate Yang Zhilin in 2023, is betting on the open-weight Kimi K3, released July 16 and instantly swarmed by users (the company briefly paused subscriptions), plus the developer traction of earlier K2.x models that made Kimi a fixture near the top of OpenRouter's usage ranks. The revenue push caps one of the fastest funding arcs of the Chinese AI cycle: a $3.5 billion round at a $35 billion valuation in July, following $2 billion at $20 billion in May.

The ambition is strategically loaded. Moonshot is among the Chinese labs Washington has accused of systematically extracting Claude exchanges through API distillation — 190 million of them, per this week's reporting — and talks with Microsoft, Amazon and Google over hosting K3 on US clouds would plant its monetization squarely inside the American market. A $2 billion run rate would make Kimi the first open-weights-first Chinese model to monetize at global-lab scale.
