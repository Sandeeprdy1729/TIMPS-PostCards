OpenAI Plans to Buy Google TPUs as Memory Crunch Deepens

Diversification from Nvidia takes a major step forward, while SK Hynix locks up majority of 2027-2028 HBM output through 2030.
>50% 2027-2028 HBM locked

OpenAI is reportedly planning to purchase Google's Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) directly, moving beyond its current rental arrangement as it seeks to diversify its compute supply away from Nvidia. The move comes as demand for AI accelerators intensifies across the industry.

SK Hynix told analysts on its Q3 call that memory will remain undersupplied through at least 2030, signing long-term agreements with 14 major customers (including global cloud providers) that already cover more than 50% of its total production capacity for 2027 and 2028. The company posted record quarterly revenue above 100 trillion won on soaring HBM demand.

The AI infrastructure race is evolving into a multi-vendor reality. With HBM locked up years in advance, compute diversification has shifted from nice-to-have to existential necessity.

OpenAI Ships GPT-6.1 Terra, Liquid AI Debuts LFM3 Family

Faster, cheaper computer-use models hit the API as open-weight multimodal options proliferate.
$1/$5 Terra per 1M tokens

OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Terra, described as fast and capable for computer use at scale. Priced at $1/1M input and $5/1M output tokens, Terra targets agentic workflows where speed and cost are critical. The company also reports strong performance in scientific research contexts.

Liquid AI introduced the LFM3 family of six open-weight models in the 2B parameter range covering text, vision, audio, omni, and calibrated decisions on a shared backbone. Every model supports single-GPU fine-tuning, with the text variant offering a 256K context window and fitting in ~1GB at 4-bit precision.

The model landscape is bifurcating: proprietary labs compete on price-performance for agentic use cases while efficient open models continue to democratize local deployment.

Oracle Goes All-In on AI, Nvidia-Meta Expand Partnership

ChatGPT and Codex land in Oracle apps as $40B SpaceX financing and massive Meta equity raises underscore infrastructure appetite.
$40B SpaceX compute financing

At Oracle AI World, Oracle announced ChatGPT and Codex will be embedded across Oracle business applications and databases, with new offerings including Oracle AI Workspace and App Composer. OCI will also add Google TPUs alongside Nvidia GPUs, with Vera Rubin systems becoming generally available.

Nvidia will invest $3 billion in Meta's planned equity fundraise as Meta expands deployment of Nvidia Vera Rubin and Vera CPUs. Meta raised its common stock offering to $25 billion with an additional $12.5 billion in convertible preferreds. Separately, SpaceX secured ~$40 billion in financing from Apollo to purchase AI processors and lease them back as it expands compute infrastructure.

AI infrastructure financing continues to scale to unprecedented levels, with creative structures (SPVs, leases, equity raises) enabling the massive buildout regardless of near-term profitability concerns.

Google DeepMind Unveils Genie 4 With Native Audio

Real-time interactive world simulation takes a leap forward with music, dialogue, and natural-language steering.
Real-time interactive simulation

Google DeepMind unveiled Genie 4, a real-time interactive world model that natively generates music, ambient audio and dialogue alongside simulated scenes. Users can steer evolving events via natural language, with Gemini 3.8 Flash acting as an in-loop guiding agent that breaks goals into steps and tracks progress.

The model is coming to trusted testers first via Project Genie for Google AI Ultra subscribers, with broader API access planned. Genie 4 represents significant progress in making world models more interactive and multimodal, though challenges remain around object permanence, physics consistency, and long-term coherence.

World models are moving from research curiosities to interactive agents. The addition of native audio and dialogue brings them closer to useful simulation environments for training and creative applications.

Anthropic Expands GSA Deal, Governments Eye AI Job Cuts

Federal discount program grows as Netherlands plans major public-sector automation.
40% government discount

Anthropic expanded its GSA agreement to federal, state and local governments with a 40% discount across Claude Enterprise, Claude for Government, and the Claude API. The company estimates savings could reach $1 billion, with roughly 500 U.S. government organizations now using Claude.

The Netherlands plans to eliminate 25,000 government jobs as part of savings associated with AI adoption — over 15% of its civil service workforce. Meanwhile, Senator Elizabeth Warren sent Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei a four-page letter demanding answers by November 3 about concentration risk, valuation, and safety disclosures tied to the upcoming IPO.

AI is moving from augmentation to automation at scale in the public sector. The policy debate is shifting from hypothetical job displacement to concrete headcount decisions and IPO scrutiny.
