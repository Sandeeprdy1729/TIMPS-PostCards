Neoclouds Shut Out of the Bond Market as AI’s Cheap-Money Era Ends

CoreWeave, Nebius and Nscale have effectively lost access to debt markets in recent weeks, as lenders demand tougher terms from the companies building AI’s data centers.
3 neoclouds priced out

CoreWeave, Nebius and Nscale have effectively been shut out of the bond market in recent weeks, according to the Wall Street Journal, as lenders and investors demand stricter terms and higher compensation for backing the AI build-out. The three are the purest plays on renting GPU capacity to AI labs, and all of them fund enormous data-center construction with borrowed money.

The shift is not that capital has vanished — it is that the price of it has changed. Investors are getting choosier about their bets as the build-out enters a new phase, one where the question is no longer whether demand exists but whether the cash flows arrive fast enough to service the debt taken on to meet it.

For two years the binding constraint on AI was chips. This week it became the cost of capital. When the companies that exist purely to finance GPUs can no longer borrow, the entire stack above them — labs, model training runs, and the leases that underwrite them — starts to reprice.

S&P Cuts Meta’s Outlook to Negative as AI Capex Swallows Its Cash Flow

The rating agency kept Meta at AA- but flagged “uncertainty” over whether its AI spending will ever earn its keep. The stock fell 15% in October, its worst month in four years.
-15% META in October

S&P Global Ratings revised Meta’s outlook from stable to negative on Friday, holding the credit rating at AA- but warning of “uncertainty” around the company’s elevated capital expenditure and its negative free cash flow. The agency also questioned Meta’s ability to monetize its AI investments relative to peers that are spending just as heavily on data centers.

Meta has rolled out its Muse AI agent app and related foundation models, but S&P said it “is unclear when these revenue streams will generate material contributions that exceed their rising costs over the next few years.” The shares fell 15% across October — their worst month in four years — as the market repriced the cost of staying in the race.

Credit raters are now asking the question equity investors have spent a year avoiding: at what point does spending money faster than you earn it become a balance-sheet problem rather than a growth strategy?

OpenAI’s Mega-Round Shrinks as Investors Trim Their Commitments

Roughly $11B has been committed against a $30B target, and the round is now reported at about $20B at a $1.25T valuation — down from $30B at $1.4T.
$11B committed so far

OpenAI has secured roughly $11 billion in commitments for its latest funding round, less than half of its initial $30 billion target, and expects to raise more over the coming weeks, according to the Wall Street Journal. The Financial Times reports the company is now seeking about $20 billion at a $1.25 trillion valuation — down from $30 billion at $1.4 trillion — as some investors cut the size of their cheques amid a broader AI selloff.

The retrenchment is selective rather than total. Inference startup Baseten is in the market for about $2 billion across two tranches valuing it at $26 billion and $30 billion, up sharply from $13 billion in June. Money is still chasing the picks and shovels; it is simply less willing to pay any price for the headline asset.

A round that shrinks by a third while the valuation falls by a tenth is the market’s way of saying the growth story is intact but the price of admission was wrong. Watch who fills the gap — and on what terms.

Tampered Claude Code Extension Backdoors Cursor’s Marketplace for 11 Hours

A malicious build of Anthropic’s extension shipped a persistent SSH backdoor to macOS and Windows machines. Cursor says up to 5,000 users were affected.
11 hrs exposure window

Between roughly 2:27am and 1:35pm PT on October 29, a maliciously modified version of Anthropic’s Claude Code extension was available in the Cursor VS Code Extension Marketplace. Versions 2.1.312 and 2.1.313 ran code that appended an attacker’s public key — labelled svc-telemetry — to ~/.ssh/authorized_keys, installing a persistent SSH backdoor on macOS and Windows machines. Reuters reports up to 5,000 users were affected.

Anthropic said it did not build or publish the tampered build. Cursor pulled it and published detection and remediation scripts that remove the extension, the persistence mechanisms, the tunnel processes and the attacker’s key. Anyone who installed or updated the extension inside that window should check their authorized keys immediately.

The package registry is the new perimeter. A trusted name, a trusted marketplace and a version bump were all an attacker needed — and the window was measured in hours, not days. Every agent toolchain now inherits this risk.

Intel’s Worst Day Since the 1970s Wipes $12B Off the Government’s Stake

Shares closed down 28.58% after guidance blamed an Israel plant shutdown and a soft PC market — a first real test of Lip-Bu Tan’s turnaround.
-28.58% Friday close

Intel shares plummeted 28.58% on Friday — their worst single day since the 1970s — after the chipmaker issued weaker-than-expected guidance, citing a plant shutdown in Israel and continued softness in the PC market. The drop wiped roughly $12 billion off the value of the US government’s 10% stake, and about $6 billion off Nvidia’s holding.

It is a sharp reversal for CEO Lip-Bu Tan, who was hired in March 2025 to revive a company that missed the early years of the AI boom. Intel shares had been up about 150% this year heading into the report on demand for CPUs in agentic AI workloads. D.A. Davidson downgraded the stock to neutral on Friday under the title “Not fixed yet.”

Intel’s recovery was priced as a completed story. One quarter of guidance just turned it back into an open question — and the government’s balance sheet is now along for the ride.

Company Watch of the Day
COREWEAVE
CoreWeave is the clearest bellwether for the AI build-out’s financing layer. It rents GPU capacity on multi-year contracts and funds the underlying data centers with debt — which makes it exquisitely sensitive to exactly what just changed. If lenders are demanding tougher terms and higher yields from neoclouds, CoreWeave’s growth math gets harder: every new megawatt costs more to finance, and every contract has to clear a higher bar. The company is not alone — Nebius and Nscale face the same wall — but CoreWeave’s scale makes it the one to watch. Its next bond or loan pricing will tell you whether this week was a wobble or a regime change.
