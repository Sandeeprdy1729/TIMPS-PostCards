Europe · The Sovereign Bet

Europe Plans a €50 Billion Bet on Its Own AI Champions

Brussels wants a sovereign fund that takes bigger, longer, riskier stakes in AI and quantum companies — and hopes private money follows at six to one.
€50B target size of the proposed Sovereign Tech Equity Fund — about a tenth of what the Commission estimates full tech sovereignty costs

The European Union is drawing up plans for a Sovereign Tech Equity Fund that could mobilise up to €50 billion ($54.8 billion), according to the Financial Times, which cited two officials involved in the discussions. The fund would make larger, riskier and longer-term investments than existing EU financing schemes, with AI and quantum computing as the main targets. “These are long-term, stable investments in risky stuff,” one official said. “It’s the opposite of what the EU usually does, which is exactly why we need it.” Reuters said it could not immediately verify the report.

The plan would use EU money as seed capital to pull in private investors at as much as six euros for every euro committed. Tech chief Henna Virkkunen discussed the idea with the European Investment Bank last week; the bank could supply financial expertise without carrying the investment risk itself. Like the existing €5 billion Scaleup Europe Fund, managed by EQT, the new fund would be run by an independent manager so investment decisions stay out of politics. Operational details are due in February, when EU leaders meet at Alden Biesen.

The Commission is also due to present a quantum strategy on Wednesday. Europe leads the world in quantum scientific publications and is home to about a third of quantum start-ups, Virkkunen said at Slush last week, but in 2025 US quantum start-ups raised about €7 billion against €1.1 billion in the EU. That gap — research strength, commercial weakness — is the problem the fund is meant to solve. It follows Mario Draghi’s argument that Europe’s inability to fund and scale innovators is a central reason it trails the US and China.

Europe is not trying to out-spend Silicon Valley; it is trying to build a balance sheet that can stay inside a company for a decade. The six-to-one ambition is the real test: public seed money only works if private capital believes the fund will be run on commercial terms. An independent manager and a February timetable are sensible, but €50 billion is a target, not an appropriation. Watch what the EU budget actually puts in, who gets the first cheques, and whether “sovereign” means owning stakes or merely hosting them.

Caveats. The plan comes from two officials quoted by the FT; the Commission has not published a formal proposal, and Reuters could not independently verify it. Size, leverage and launch date are all still under discussion. The €7 billion versus €1.1 billion venture comparison comes from Virkkunen’s own speech. Watch the February details, the quantum strategy itself, and the budget negotiations that would have to supply the seed.

02 · MATHEMATICS
Models · The Speed Limit

OpenAI Says It Has Pinned the Exact Speed Limit of Matrix Multiplication

A new preprint claims the exponent is exactly 9/4 over every field — an upper bound plus a matching lower bound. It is unreviewed, and the machine-checked proof covers less than the full claim.
9/4 the claimed exact exponent — about n2.25 operations, with no circuit able to do better

OpenAI published a preprint on November 24 titled “The matrix multiplication exponent is nine fourths,” claiming that the exponent ω is exactly 9/4 over every field. It joins an upper bound already on record from an October paper to a new lower bound: for every n ≥ 2, a formal arithmetic circuit multiplying two n × n matrices needs at least ⌈n9/4/2⌉ nonscalar multiplication or division gates, even when additions are free. The previous best upper bound was about 2.371177, set in August.

The paper was produced by an unreleased internal model, according to reports, and posted alongside a companion preprint on tensor geometry and a Lean formalisation. The formal development proves the square exponent for division-free circuits over every field — narrower than the full paper. OpenAI’s Sebastien Bubeck wrote that the model had been working on the lower bound since October: “Nothing was known for lower bounds… This is really the kind of result that I never thought I would see in my lifetime.” He notes an older result means linear programs could then be solved in n9/4 time.

This is a statement about arithmetic circuits, not wall-clock speed. The exponent is an infimum: the algorithm costs n(9/4 + ε) for any ε > 0, and the constants are not practical. No matrix library gets faster tomorrow. What changes is the theoretical target — the long-held conjecture that ω = 2 would be false if the proof holds, and a fifty-year search would finally have a floor.

Two days after a machine-assisted Riemann proof was certified, OpenAI claims an exact answer to an older algorithms question. That cadence is the story as much as the theorem: models are now producing lower bounds, where there was little prior work to automate. Treat 9/4 as a claim, not a fact — it is a first-party preprint with no completed peer review, and the Lean check does not cover division. Watch which group first verifies the lower bound independently, and whether the argument survives being rewritten by hand.

Caveats. The paper is an unreviewed preprint; the upper bound was established earlier and extended to every field by other researchers. The formalisation covers division-free circuits only. Reports that an unreleased model did the work come from OpenAI researchers and secondary coverage, not an independent audit. There is no practical speedup and no named finite matrix size where the algorithm wins. Watch for an ECCC or arXiv verification of the lower bound.

03 · CHIPS
Memory · The Patent Moat

Samsung and SK Hynix Are Licensing the Stacking Tech They Used to Ignore

Cross-licensing deals give Korea’s memory giants access to parts of YMTC’s Xtacking — a sign the Chinese company’s patents now matter to the industry’s biggest players.
12,000+ patents filed worldwide by YMTC, including more than 4,000 US applications since 2018

Nikkei Asia reports, citing three people briefed on the matter, that Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix have agreed cross-licensing arrangements letting them use parts of Xtacking, the 3D NAND stacking technology first developed by Yangtze Memory Technologies. Xtacking builds memory cells and their controlling CMOS circuits on separate wafers, then bonds them together — a method that helps as stacks pass 400 layers. Samsung declined to comment on the licences but said bonding technology “reduces structural problems that can arise during stacking” and raises memory density. SK Hynix declined to comment; YMTC did not respond.

The patent portfolio is the point. YMTC says it has filed more than 12,000 patents worldwide; USPTO records show over 4,000 US filings since 2018. TrendForce counted 119 hybrid-bonding patents held by YMTC from 2017 to January 2024, against 83 for Samsung and 11 for SK Hynix. One industry executive told Nikkei the technology is “not that difficult” and that the main reason to cross-license is to avoid litigation. YMTC has sued Micron over patents and filed for an IPO in August through parent company CCSH.

This is what a decade of export controls produces at the margin: not a Chinese company that has replaced Samsung, but one whose intellectual property Samsung needs. Counterpoint puts YMTC’s second-quarter NAND share at 14%, up from 11% a year earlier, level with Kioxia and behind Samsung’s 28%, SK Hynix’s 19% and Micron’s 15%. Access to Xtacking could speed denser enterprise drives for AI data centres even as the Korean companies keep their scale advantage.

Patents have become a tool of industrial policy. A cross-licence is a quiet admission that the engineering gap has narrowed enough for legal risk to matter more than pride. For buyers of enterprise SSDs, the benefit will show up in products, not press releases. Watch whether the deals translate into faster layer-count roadmaps, and what happens when YMTC’s IPO puts a price on that portfolio.

Caveats. Nikkei relies on unnamed sources; none of the three companies confirmed the cross-licences, and the financial terms and exact technologies covered are undisclosed. Patent counts measure filings, not quality or enforceability, and the 119-versus-83 comparison is TrendForce’s. Market-share figures are Counterpoint estimates. Watch for product announcements and the IPO prospectus.

04 · GEOPOLITICS
Courts · The Split Verdict

Huawei Is Guilty on 10 Counts in Brooklyn. The Big One Didn’t Stick.

A jury convicted the Chinese giant of fraud, sanctions violations and stealing trade secrets, but cleared it of racketeering — the charge that would have branded it a criminal enterprise.
10 of 12 criminal counts on which the jury found Huawei and three subsidiaries guilty; sentencing is set for September 14, 2027

A federal jury in Brooklyn convicted Huawei Technologies and three subsidiaries on 10 of 12 counts on Tuesday, after a trial lasting more than two months. The guilty verdicts cover bank and wire fraud, conspiracy to steal trade secrets, conspiracy to violate the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, a substantive IEEPA violation, conspiracy to defraud the United States and money-laundering conspiracy. The jury acquitted the company of racketeering conspiracy — the top count — and of conspiracy to obstruct justice.

Prosecutors said Huawei concealed its control of Skycom and lied to banks about its business in Iran. They showed jurors a video of an employee putting part of a T-Mobile testing robot into a backpack, and presented evidence of efforts to obtain Cisco source code, Quintel antenna technology and a circuit board from CNEX. The defence called the case a collection of unrelated incidents across two decades. More than 60 prosecution witnesses testified; the defence called 15.

Huawei welcomed the racketeering acquittal, saying it “confirms that these allegations lack factual basis”, while saying the remaining charges also lack legal and factual merit. Its lawyer said the company expects to appeal. US Attorney Joseph Nocella called the verdict “a step towards accountability.” Judge Ann Donnelly will sentence the companies on September 14, 2027; they face fines, forfeiture and restitution, though the acquittal could materially change the exposure.

The split verdict is the only honest headline. A company can be a convicted fraudster and not a criminal enterprise; both things are true here. The financial penalty is still to be decided, and the business Washington spent eight years trying to constrain is now central to China’s AI push. Watch the sentence, the appeal, and whether this changes how Huawei is treated in markets that never adopted the US restrictions.

Caveats. The verdicts are guilty findings on specific counts, not a finding that every allegation in the 2018 indictment was proved. Sentencing is ten months away and no penalty has been set. Huawei’s statement addressed the RICO acquittal without discussing the ten convictions. The account of the evidence comes from court reporting and the Justice Department’s press release. Watch the sentencing memorandum and the appeal.

Hardware · The Memory Bill

HP Sold More PCs and Earned Less. AI PCs Are Now 60% of Shipments.

Revenue hit $16 billion and beat the Street. A 3% Personal Systems margin and lower cash-flow guidance told the other half of the story.
$16.0B fourth-quarter revenue, up 9.3% year over year and above the $15.3 billion consensus

HP Inc. reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $16.0 billion on Tuesday, up 9.3% from a year earlier and above the roughly $15.3 billion analysts expected. Non-GAAP earnings came in at $0.83 per share, down from $0.93 but ahead of the $0.74 consensus; both GAAP and non-GAAP figures include a $0.15 benefit from tariff refunds. The board raised the quarterly dividend to $0.31 from $0.30.

Personal Systems revenue rose 14% to $11.8 billion, with commercial PCs up 23% and consumer down 7%; total PC units fell 29%. Segment operating margin fell to 3.0% from 5.8% as component costs climbed. Interim CEO Bruce Broussard said AI PCs reached nearly 60% of shipments, well ahead of the 50% target, and CFO Karen Parkhill said the Windows 11 refresh is “mostly behind us” — the next upgrade cycle has to be about AI at the edge.

Full-year revenue was $60.5 billion, up 9.5%. But fiscal 2027 guidance calls for non-GAAP EPS of $3.15 to $3.45 — a midpoint essentially flat on the $3.33 just reported — and free cash flow of $2.9 billion to $3.1 billion, below the $3.3 billion generated this year. Inventory stood at 84 days, up 11 days. HP also said it will exclude stock-based compensation from non-GAAP results next year, a change it says would have added $0.38 a share to fiscal 2026.

The PC market is growing revenue by raising prices while shipping fewer machines. That is a memory-cost story, and it runs straight through the NAND and DRAM suppliers above. AI PCs are real — 60% of shipments is not a label — but a shift toward premium commercial machines does not automatically restore margin when the components inside cost more. Watch whether incoming CEO Emma Walmsley, who starts January 4, can turn mix into profit.

Caveats. The quarter ended October 31 and the figures are HP’s reported results; non-GAAP measures exclude items HP chooses. The tariff-refund benefit flatters both years’ comparisons. The AI PC share is HP’s own definition and is not an industry-wide measure. The link to memory pricing is our read across HP’s commentary and the chip stories above, not a management claim. Watch the next call and the January 4 handover.
