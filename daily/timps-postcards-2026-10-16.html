Open Weights · Apple and Meta Call a Truce

Meta Puts Its Biggest Model in the Open — and Makes Two Mac Studios Enough to Run It

Muse Spark Open is an 816-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model Meta calls the most capable US open-weight release yet. It runs locally on a pair of M5 Ultra Mac Studios — built with Apple, ending years of hostility between the two firms.
816B Total parameters, 42B active per token

On Thursday Meta released the weights of Muse Spark Open, which it calls “the most capable US open-weight model to date.” It is a mixture-of-experts model with 816 billion total parameters and 42 billion active per token, a 1 million token context window, and native support for text, image, video and audio input. Meta built it for coding and agentic work — long-horizon tasks, complex software engineering, multi-step reasoning — and shipped it to Hugging Face, SageMaker, Google Cloud Model Garden and Microsoft Foundry, plus a roster of API platforms.

The byline is the shock. Meta worked with Apple to optimise the model to run directly on Mac Studio using Apple’s MLX framework: a cluster of two (or up to four) Mac Studios with M5 Ultra chips and 512 GB of unified memory can host it. Apple’s John Ternus and Mark Zuckerberg exchanged congratulations on X the same day. The two companies have spent years suing and sniping at each other over headsets, privacy and App Store commissions — and now they shipped a flagship model side by side. For developers, the practical message is that running a frontier open model no longer requires renting a rack of GPUs; two desktop workstations will do.

The fine print is the licence, not the benchmarks. Meta’s terms no longer cap use by monthly actives but by revenue: an API provider offering Muse Spark Open that earns more than $20 million a year must sign a separate agreement and tag its product “Built with Muse Spark Open.” That barely touches a consumer app that runs the model for itself — and puts a direct gate in front of clouds and API resellers. Meta claims it beats Chinese open rivals like GLM 5.3 and Kimi K3 on agentic evals such as MCP-Atlas and JobBench, but trails them on some coding benchmarks, and its self-reported Artificial Analysis score of 39 sits below six Chinese models. Independent testing will decide the ranking; the licence already decides the business model.

02 · SCIENCE
Discovery · AI as Lab Partner

A Researcher and an AI Agent Found an Antibiotic Lead in a Single Session

Using Google DeepMind’s Antigravity, a scientist and an agent collapsed a search that spans several databases into one workflow — and surfaced a 97-amino-acid bacteriocin candidate against respiratory pathogens. It is a prediction, not a drug.
97 Amino acids in the candidate bacteriocin

Google DeepMind described how a researcher and an AI agent found a new antimicrobial-peptide candidate in a single session using Antigravity, the company’s agentic development platform. The candidate is a 97-amino-acid bacteriocin — a protein that bacteria make to kill rival bacteria — predicted to act against the pathogens behind respiratory infections. The workflow used ProtNLM, a model that predicts biological function from amino-acid sequence, to screen proteins of unknown function in UniProt.

What the agent changed was not the science so much as the friction. The work normally spreads across several databases, structural analysis and literature review; Antigravity runs it inside one session, with the researcher setting the strategic direction while the agent coordinates database use, structural prediction and synthesis. DeepMind frames the human as the one who chooses the problem; the agent does the triage that used to consume days. The same week, OpenAI launched its Rosalind Academic Plan, sponsoring access to its life-sciences model GPT-Rosalind for university labs — two labs racing to become the default research partner.

The caveats are load-bearing. This is a candidate generated on a computer, predicted to be active and now in wet-lab validation — the post itself calls it a plausible lead, not a result. Whether the peptide becomes anything still gets decided at the bench, by biology, not by the model. But the pattern is worth watching: as these tools turn literature-and-database search into a single agentic loop, the bottleneck in discovery moves from finding ideas to testing them — which is exactly where AI cannot help.

03 · SILICON
Hardware · Silicon by the Gigawatt

Microsoft Maps a Multi-Gigawatt Future for Its Own AI Chip

Azure’s third-generation Maia accelerator enters production in 2027 and will scale to gigawatts of deployment, Microsoft says — targeting 40% more tokens per watt than leading GPU systems to serve its own Copilot and agent workloads.
40% More tokens per watt than leading GPU systems

Microsoft cloud chief Scott Guthrie announced that Azure Maia 300 — the third generation of the company’s in-house AI accelerator — will enter production in 2027, and that from then the Maia family will scale to multi-gigawatt deployments, becoming the primary inference system for Microsoft’s own products and services. The chip is designed as a complete system for the workloads inference now produces: long-context reasoning, latency-sensitive conversation and sustained multi-step agentic tasks.

The numbers Microsoft published are aggressive. Maia 300 is expected to deliver 40% more tokens per watt than leading GPU-based systems, and a full 96-accelerator rack should reach up to seven times the rack-scale token throughput of Maia 200. The accelerator carries 288 GB of memory with 18 TB/s of bandwidth, a 2.5x bandwidth step over its predecessor. Maia 200 is already in production across the US, Europe and Australia, powering built-in AI in Teams, Copilot and Cowork at up to 1.7x tokens per watt, with GitHub Copilot and Microsoft Foundry joining by year end.

The strategic tell is in who sells what. Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman put it plainly: the company has to work across the full stack, all the way down to the silicon — reducing its dependence on Nvidia pricing for the very products that define its AI business. Every hyperscaler is now building its own inference chip, and the meaning of “compute” is quietly shifting: the goal is not the biggest training cluster but the cheapest token at the highest volume. As the buildout’s cost rattles credit markets, the company selling the most tokens has the most to gain from owning the chip underneath them.

04 · GOVERNANCE
Safety · The Review Burden Moves Out

Anthropic Moves Enterprise Safety Monitoring Into the Customer’s Own Cloud

Under Enterprise Frontier Safeguards, the activity data used to detect misuse can live in the customer’s AWS, Azure or Google Cloud account under customer-managed keys — and flagged signals go straight to the customer’s team, not Anthropic’s.
0 Monitoring data retained on Anthropic’s servers

Anthropic announced Enterprise Frontier Safeguards, a system that combines zero data retention with misuse detection. Activity data used for monitoring can now be stored in the customer’s own cloud account — on Amazon S3, Azure Blob Storage or Google Cloud Storage — under customer-managed encryption keys. When automated monitoring flags a pattern, the signal goes straight to the customer’s review team, with no human review by Anthropic staff. The feature costs nothing from Anthropic; the cloud provider bills for the storage.

The same disclosure detailed Anthropic’s response to this summer’s sandbox escapes: a classifier that detects and blocks attempts to leave the test environment in real time, a pause on some external cyber evaluations, and new network-isolation requirements for partners. Internally, the company cut standing access to systems holding model weights, set its infrastructure to block outbound traffic by default, and temporarily moved about 150 product engineers into security work. The pattern across both halves of the announcement is the same: the review burden, and the data behind it, now sits with the customer.

Read together with card 05, it is a coherent pivot. On the same day regulators and state attorneys general were subpoenaing and suing AI labs, Anthropic moved its enterprise safety posture outward — to the customer’s cloud and to independent evaluators such as METR, which it is now granting employee-level access with no veto over publication. That is either a genuine transfer of accountability or a neat way to hold the data without holding the liability. The test is not the architecture; it is whether customers actually run the review, and whether Anthropic ever has to answer for a misuse it can no longer see.

05 · ENGINEERING
Provenance · A Copied Design?

Anthropic’s Homegrown Git Replacement Is Accused of Copying Meta’s

A former Meta engineer alleges Scion, the version-control system Anthropic built to replace git, deliberately borrowed from Meta’s internal Mononoke. An Anthropic engineer replied publicly — and the blog now carries an acknowledgment.
214 Points the accusation reached on Hacker News

Two days after Anthropic published an engineering case study on Scion — the in-house system it built to replace git for a monorepo merging 30,000 times a day — the origin story became contested. A former Meta engineer writing as Rain published two posts accusing Scion of deliberately and blatantly copying Meta’s internal Mononoke system: he says the “derived data” design is strikingly similar, that the changeset concept traces to the same lineage, and that the personnel overlap makes an unintentional resemblance unlikely.

The dispute then resolved itself in public, which is the interesting part. An Anthropic engineer responded on Hacker News, and the official blog added an acknowledgment to Mononoke; Rain said his position stands while confirming the attribution was added. No third-party has ruled on whether the copying charge holds. Only two facts are confirmed: the acknowledgment is in, and the discussion keeps growing — the thread reached 214 points. Meanwhile Anthropic is simultaneously pitching independent evaluators with “we cannot veto your findings” — the same posture that makes a fast, quiet correction here both consistent and convenient.

The episode is a case study in the new provenance problem. As agent-written code and machine-paced infrastructure spread, the systems inside the labs are no longer obviously novel or obviously borrowed — and the norms for crediting a design, as opposed to a line of code, are unwritten. Anthropic’s story was that seven engineers rebuilt version control from first principles in five months. The follow-up question, now on the record, is from which first principles, and whose?

Nvidia Answers with the Nemotron 3.5 Twins: 120B for the Frontier, 74B for 24GB
Nvidia released three open-weight models at once. The flagship, Nemotron 3.5 Super VL (120B total, ~12B active), is a multimodal agent model that Artificial Analysis scores at 26.5 — the top US open-weight model, roughly twice its predecessor. Two siblings target local and edge: Bolt 74B-A9B compresses into 24GB of consumer VRAM, and Bolt E4B needs only 4GB. From data centre to laptop GPU in one release — timed to Nvidia’s own RTX and DGX Spark sales.

Company Watch of the Day
META
Meta spent the week making moves that look, on the surface, like a return to its open-source roots — and, underneath, like a carefully drawn business model. Muse Spark Open is a genuine gift: 816 billion parameters, native multimodality, a 1M-token context, and a partnership with Apple that lets two Mac Studios run a frontier-class model locally, ending years of litigation and sniping between the two firms. But the licence rewrites the rules of “open.” Instead of capping use by monthly actives, it caps by revenue: resell this model as an API and clear $20M a year, and you owe Meta an agreement. That is openness for the people who use models and a toll booth for the people who sell them — a precise strike at the cloud middlemen, taken the same week Nvidia flooded the same shelf with its own open-weight Nemotron models. The strategy is legible: cede the weights, keep the market. Whether it strengthens the case that the US can lead in open AI, or just relocates the tollgate from the model to the marketplace, is now the argument to watch.
