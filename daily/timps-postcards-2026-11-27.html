SAP Plans to Cut 10,000 Jobs. AI Is the Reason.

Bloomberg reports a second round of 10,000 cuts in two years. This one is tied to a rewrite of how the software giant builds and sells its products.
10,000 positions under consideration — at least 9% of SAP’s global workforce, with the final number still subject to labour negotiations

Bloomberg reported on Thursday that SAP is preparing to eliminate at least 10,000 positions, or more than 9% of its workforce, to reshape its software business around artificial intelligence. People familiar with the discussions said product development, engineering and sales would see the largest cuts, and that other departments had been asked to find savings. The plan has not been finalised. The Financial Times reported that employee representatives have not yet been consulted.

Germany is part of the reason the number is not settled. SAP and a labour council at home have negotiated an employment guarantee that runs through 2028, which may push the reductions towards other markets. The company’s headcount climbed back to about 113,000 after its last major restructuring, so this would be its second cut of roughly 10,000 roles in about two years. Chief Financial Officer Dominik Asam told investors in May that headcount was expected to decline.

The stated logic is AI. CEO Christian Klein wants to change how SAP develops and sells software, embedding tools such as Joule Work, an interface that pulls its applications into one place, and Joule Assistants, a suite of agents the company says will use more than 200 subagents to automate repetitive business tasks. Leadership is changing too: two of six executive board members are expected to leave in March, including sales chief Thomas Saueressig, who is moving to Ionos. The cuts follow a quarter in which sales and adjusted profit beat expectations and the €24.11 billion cloud backlog came in ahead of consensus.

This is the clearest statement yet of what “AI-first” costs inside an enterprise software company. SAP sells the software that automates back-office work; now it is rebuilding that software with fewer people who used to build it. The strong quarter matters: this is not a company cutting because it is shrinking, but one cutting while it says demand is strong. Watch the January announcement window, the final headcount, and how much of the reduction lands outside Germany.

Caveats. The plan comes from people familiar with the discussions, not an SAP announcement. SAP says it continuously reviews its organisation and that “no decisions have been made regarding any further measures.” The 113,000 headcount figure comes from the Financial Times. Timing and geography are unresolved.

Apple Took 57% of a Shrinking US Phone Market

Counterpoint says US smartphone shipments fell 9% in Q3 as memory costs squeezed low-end Android. Apple was the only major vendor to gain share.
57% of US smartphone shipments in Q3, up from 50% a year earlier, as the total market fell 9%

Counterpoint Research published its Q3 US smartphone report on Thursday, and the headline number is a contraction. Shipments fell 9% year over year. Inside that decline, Apple’s share jumped from 50% to 57%. It was the only major maker to avoid a year-over-year fall in shipment volume. Samsung was the only Android vendor to improve its share, gaining one point to 25%, helped by a range spanning flagships and the low-end Galaxy A-series.

The split is a story about memory. Counterpoint says rising memory costs hit the low-end Android segment hardest during the quarter, forcing some vendors to raise prices more than once. Apple held iPhone pricing largely stable for much of the year, delaying broad increases until the iPhone 18 Pro lineup launched in September. Carriers felt the downturn too: smartphone sales across the three major US operators fell a combined 4% year over year.

The outlook is not brighter. Counterpoint expects memory costs to stay elevated and carriers to be more selective with holiday promotions, leaving Q4 sales weak despite it being the strongest quarter of the year. The same pressure showed up elsewhere in this week’s news: a separate report put memory prices up as much as tenfold in a year as AI demand pulled supply towards data centres.

A memory shortage is a tax on the cheapest phones. When components cost more, the vendor with the most expensive, most loyal product can absorb it and the vendor selling to price-sensitive buyers cannot. Apple’s share gain here is less a triumph of new demand than a redistribution of a shrinking pie. Watch whether the holiday quarter confirms it, and whether the low end passes the cost on to buyers.

Caveats. Counterpoint’s figures are estimates of shipments, not sales to consumers, and cover one quarter in one market. The Q4 weakness is a projection. Share gains can reflect competitors’ declines as much as Apple’s own strength.

Geopolitics · The Export Line

“The US Is Not ASML’s Boss,” Dutch Minister Says

The Netherlands will fight a US bill extending chip-tool curbs from EUV to immersion DUV and servicing. China is roughly a fifth of ASML’s revenue.
20% of ASML’s revenue comes from China — the share the Dutch government says a US export bill puts at risk

Dutch Trade Minister Sjoerd Sjoerdsma told parliament on Thursday that the Netherlands will push back against the Match Act moving through the US Congress. His formulation was blunt: “the United States is not the boss of Veldhoven,” the town where ASML is headquartered. Bloomberg reported the comments. Sjoerdsma called the bill a serious threat to Europe’s most valuable company and said Washington’s interest in ASML’s capabilities was “a kind of jealousy” that could not be satisfied by moving the company abroad.

The bill is designed to press allies to tighten exports of chipmaking equipment to China. Restrictions on the most advanced extreme ultraviolet (EUV) machines are already in place. The Match Act would extend them to sales of immersion deep ultraviolet (DUV) systems used at older process nodes, and to maintenance and servicing of equipment already installed. China accounts for roughly 20% of ASML’s total revenue. The Dutch government has been lobbying Washington for months to stop the measure being attached to the National Defense Authorization Act as a rider.

Sjoerdsma declined to name countermeasures but warned that even if the bill passed, his government would do everything possible to prevent implementation, and would respond forcefully if it were enforced. ASML’s CEO Christophe Fouquet made the industry case separately this week: export controls should protect the most advanced technology without cutting China off entirely, because a frustrated Beijing would only double down on domestic rivals. He argues that selling older machines preserves a generation gap and keeps China dependent on Western hardware, a position ASML can hold while it remains the sole supplier of the machines needed for advanced chips.

The fight is no longer only about the newest machine. It is about who controls the installed base — the tools already in fabs, and the service contracts that keep them running. For the Netherlands, this is both a revenue question and a sovereignty question. For Washington, an ally that refuses to enforce a rule is a rule that does not exist. Watch the NDAA markup and whether the DUV and servicing language survives.

Caveats. The Match Act is a proposal, not law, and was described as having little chance of passing on its own. The minister’s remarks were reported through Bloomberg and relayed by other outlets. No countermeasures have been announced. Fouquet’s argument is the seller’s case, not an independent assessment of export-control effectiveness.

Hardware · The Laser-Free Bet

Avicena Raised $100 Million to Wire AI Chips With Light

SK Hynix, Samsung and AMD joined a Series C for a microLED link that needs no lasers. The first 1 Tbps kits shipped in August.
$100M+ Series C led by InterVest, with SK hynix Ventures, Samsung Catalyst Fund and AMD Ventures among the investors

Avicena, a Sunnyvale startup that links AI chips with tiny LEDs instead of lasers, announced on Tuesday that it had raised more than $100 million in Series C funding. InterVest led the round. Tiger Global, Cerberus Capital Management, SK hynix Ventures, Samsung Catalyst Fund and AMD Ventures took part, along with Prosperity7, Mitsubishi Chemical’s Diamond Edge Ventures and Hitachi Ventures. The company also has a new chief executive: Kevin Pope succeeds Marco Chisari, and InterVest director John Dongwoo Ha joins the board.

The technology is called LightBundle. It uses dense arrays of microLEDs and silicon photodetectors connected by multicore fibre. Rather than pushing a few channels very fast, it spreads data across many slower ones, which Avicena says reduces the need for data-rate conversion and complex signal processing. Because there is no laser, the platform can sit close to compute and memory chips. Avicena pitches links inside and between chip packages, and across accelerator systems. It began shipping 1 Tbps evaluation kits in August and showed a connectorised version at the ECOC optics conference in September.

The investor list is the signal. SK hynix has said the memory shortage will last until 2030, and its venture arm said it hopes to work with Avicena on future memory interconnects. Samsung’s fund pointed to energy and data movement as the limits on the AI build-out; AMD’s venture arm made the same case about moving data between compute and memory. The money goes to hiring, product development and engineering, with a first product planned for next year.

The bottleneck is no longer just arithmetic, it is the wires between the chips. Copper hits limits on reach, density and power, and lasers bring their own heat and complexity. Avicena’s bet is that proximity matters more than raw per-channel speed. Three of the biggest names in memory and compute just paid to find out. Watch the first product, and whether the evaluation kits turn into design wins.

Caveats. The technical claims come from the company and its investors. Evaluation kits are not production parts, and the first product is still a year away. The round was announced by Avicena; no valuation was disclosed. The memory-shortage-to-2030 figure is SK hynix’s own forecast.

Markets · The Share Giveaway

Musk Pledges a SpaceX Share to Every US Child. Then Another Every Year.

The gift rides on Trump Accounts, tax-advantaged investment accounts for children. SpaceX shares jumped 7.5% in overnight trading.
~70M Trump Accounts had been created as of October, according to the White House; family and friends have contributed more than $1 billion

Elon Musk said on X that he will donate “another share of SpaceX to all kids in America every year.” The pledge expands an announcement made on Wednesday, when SpaceX said it would give one share of its Class A stock to every active Trump Account — tax-advantaged investment accounts for American children — with Musk personally matching the gift. Secondary estimates put the two-share package near $18 billion.

The mechanics are staggered. SpaceX said its contribution would be transferred next month. Musk’s personal donation follows when his lockup period expires in June next year. The accounts launched in July; by October, nearly 70 million had been created, according to the White House. Families and friends have put in more than $1 billion, bringing total deposits from all sources to more than $9 billion, CNBC reported earlier this week.

The market reacted before a regular session opened. SpaceX shares rose 7.47% overnight on Blue Ocean ATS, an alternative trading system that runs from 8 p.m. to 4 a.m. ET, after the markets closed for Thanksgiving. The stock had closed 1.30% higher on Wednesday. Overnight volumes on an ATS are thin, so the move is a reading on sentiment in a market where SpaceX shares do not trade on a major exchange.

This is philanthropy with an equity ticker and a lockup. It hands a private-company asset to tens of millions of children at once, and it does so through a tax-advantaged wrapper that already carries the government’s imprint. Whether it is a durable gift or a one-off price signal depends on what the shares are worth when the lockup ends, and whether the annual pledge survives a few annual pledges. Watch the June transfer and the first valuation published afterwards.

Caveats. These are pledges, not completed transfers. The $18 billion figure is a secondary estimate based on a private-company share price; SpaceX does not publish a public market capitalisation. Overnight ATS prices are set in thin trading. The White House account count and deposit totals are programme figures, not audited statements.
