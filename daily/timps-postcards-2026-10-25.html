Pricing · The Price War Begins

OpenAI Undercuts Anthropic as Replit Flips Its Largest AI Spend

Anthropic discontinued a key discount, OpenAI offered 30%+ off in a deal worth over $100M/year, and Replit moved most of its spend from Anthropic to OpenAI.
$100M+ per year in spend at stake

Replit recently moved most of its AI spend from Anthropic to OpenAI after Anthropic discontinued one of its discounts, according to people familiar with the matter. OpenAI stepped in with an offer of 30%+ off in a deal worth more than $100 million per year. The switch underscores how quickly enterprise AI spending can flip when pricing terms change and alternatives are competitive.

Anthropic had been a dominant provider for many coding agents, including Replit, thanks to Claude’s strong performance on agentic tasks. But as OpenAI improves its own coding models and becomes more aggressive on price for large customers, the calculus is changing. For builders, model selection is increasingly a procurement decision: performance matters, but so does committed spend, rate limits and discounts that can swing by tens of millions of dollars.

The move also fits a broader pattern in frontier AI: hyperscalers and labs are competing less on benchmarks alone and more on enterprise contracts, with discounts used to lock in high-volume customers before they standardize on a stack. In the agent economy, the winner may not be the model with the highest score, but the one whose sales team shows up with the biggest checkbook.

02 · SPEECH
Voice AI · Talk to Me Naturally

Jack Dorsey’s Block Unveils Dessa, a Speech-to-Speech Model Trained on Human Conversation

Dessa runs locally on an M4 Max MacBook Pro or better and aims for more natural timing by learning directly from speech, not just transcribed text.
Local runs on M4 Max or better

Block unveiled Dessa, a “true speech-to-speech” AI model trained “from scratch on nothing but human conversation.” The model can run locally on a MacBook Pro M4 Max or newer. Block shared examples showing mostly natural back-and-forths, occasional awkward moments, and even a comparison with OpenAI’s GPT-Live-1 where Dessa came across as more natural.

The key difference is approach: rather than transcribing speech to text, running an LLM, then converting back to speech, Dessa learns directly from audio. The team’s goal is to capture timing, pauses and rhythm that get flattened by the text bottleneck. It can also pick up on context in conversation — one demo showed it responding with apparent emotion when told about a surprise trip to Paris.

Block frames it as curiosity-driven research into how speech works, but the implications for voice assistants are obvious. If speech-to-speech models can run locally with low latency and feel more human, they could power assistants that don’t feel like they’re reading from a script. The question is whether “more natural” is also more trustworthy, especially when it can seem emotionally engaged without actually feeling anything.

03 · ECONOMY
Productivity · No Payoff Yet

Apollo Finds No Financial Payoff From AI Outside of Tech

Despite massive investment, companies outside the tech sector have seen no financial payoff from AI so far — and for many it’s becoming a margin-hurting cost center.
Zero measurable payoff outside tech

Apollo Global Management found that outside of the tech sector, US companies have seen no financial payoff from their AI investments so far. What was once hyped as a productivity revolution is increasingly being viewed as a margin-hurting cost center. Meanwhile, venture-backed firms are burning more tokens as they race to build and scale AI products.

The gap between hype and measurable returns is becoming harder to ignore. Tech companies are seeing some benefits through cost savings and new products, but in industries like manufacturing, retail, healthcare and finance, the productivity gains remain elusive. Many companies are still in the experimentation phase, but investors are starting to ask when those investments will show up in earnings.

The finding echoes what many CFOs are privately saying: AI is expensive, adoption is messy, and the ROI timeline keeps slipping. The AI boom has so far been a capital expenditure boom, not a profit boom — and the bill is coming due.

04 · GEOPOLITICS
Satellite Warfare · Leverage Restored

Ukraine Says Starshield Is Working Normally Again After Trump Calls Musk

US officials say President Trump asked Elon Musk to restore the service after restrictions hampered Ukraine’s drone forces since October 14.
Oct 14 restrictions began

Ukraine says its military Starshield system is working normally again on October 25 after speed restrictions hampered its drone forces since October 14. US officials said President Trump asked Elon Musk to restore the service. The move comes after days of confusion over who ordered the slowdown and whether it had been lifted.

The episode underscored once again how dependent Ukraine’s war effort is on a commercial satellite network that can be throttled by a single phone call. Musk has previously said Starshield is owned and controlled by the US government, with limits decided by the military and ultimately the president — a structure that creates maximum leverage with minimum accountability.

For European allies watching nervously, the lesson is the same as with other American tech dependencies: “no kill switch” rhetoric means little when the on/off lever exists somewhere else. In modern warfare, connectivity is a weapon — and weapons controlled by others are not fully yours.

05 · DEALS
Fintech · Credit Consolidates

LSEG to Buy Octus for $3.54 Billion as Credit Data Consolidates

The London Stock Exchange Group is acquiring the New York-based credit intelligence platform from Permira and Octus’ management to strengthen its private markets presence.
$3.54B all-cash acquisition

London Stock Exchange Group (LSEG) agreed to acquire Octus, a New York-based provider of credit intelligence, data and workflow software, from Permira and Octus’ management for $3.54 billion in cash. The deal will transform LSEG’s presence in credit and private markets data as demand for intelligence on distressed debt, restructuring and private credit continues to grow.

Octus provides real-time credit news, data and analytics used by investors, law firms and advisors to track complex credit situations. As private credit has exploded into a multi-trillion-dollar asset class, the need for better data and workflow tools has grown alongside it. LSEG has been investing heavily to expand beyond its traditional exchange and index businesses into data and analytics.

The acquisition follows a wave of consolidation in financial data, as incumbents look to bolt on specialized providers to compete with Bloomberg and others in high-value niches. In credit markets, information advantage is everything — and owning the pipes to that information is becoming more valuable.

Company Watch of the Day
ANTHROPIC
Anthropic is learning the hard lesson of being the leader: when you’re on top, competitors will undercut you. The company discontinued a key discount for Replit, its largest customer in some sense, and watched as OpenAI swooped in with 30%+ off in a deal worth more than $100 million per year. For Anthropic, this is a margin problem as much as a competitive one — the company needs revenue to justify its ambitions, but deep discounts erode margins and training costs remain enormous. Claude remains one of the best models for coding agents, but in enterprise procurement, “best” has a price. Anthropic can afford to win on capability, but it may not be able to afford to win on price against a competitor willing to subsidize to regain share. The price war just started, and the margin-sensitive leader is suddenly on the defensive.
