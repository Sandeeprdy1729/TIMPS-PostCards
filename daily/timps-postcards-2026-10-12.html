World Affairs · After a 17-Day Freeze

The White House Restores UK’s Access to Frontier AI — the Freeze Lifts a Model Too Late

Asked by Washington on 24 September to hold back pre-release models, OpenAI and Anthropic complied — and Anthropic kept Claude Mythos 5.1 out of Britain’s hands. On 11 October, after lobbying from AI minister Kanishka Narayan, the directive quietly reversed.
17 Days the UK was frozen out

The Trump administration has restored the UK’s AI Security Institute (AISI) to pre-release model access, The Telegraph reported on Sunday — reversing a 24 September request that OpenAI, Anthropic and other named labs stop sharing unreleased frontier models with British testers until Washington finished its own national security review. The freeze lasted exactly 17 days, and its most visible casualty was a model: Anthropic shipped Claude Mythos 5.1 during the window restricted to US organizations only.

The reversal came after sustained lobbying from Kanishka Narayan, the UK’s AI minister, and other ministers. AISI will again review pre-release systems from Anthropic, Google DeepMind and OpenAI — the same trio the September order had paused. The bureaucratic texture matters: the hold was a request, not a law; the Office of the National Cyber Director asked the labs to wait, and the labs complied. Nothing in the public record says the underlying security review ever concluded — the access simply came back, and the condition that justified pausing it is still hanging over the arrangement.

The timing is pointed. Monday, Narayan is due to argue in a London speech that frontier labs cannot be left to police themselves, even as Britain again claims a seat at the pre-release table — while a US task force, the “Super Intelligence Force,” told the labs on 9 October that delayed disclosure of incidents “will not be tolerated.” The 17-day freeze showed two things at once: how informal this oversight layer really is, and how much of the transatlantic AI order still runs on a US unilateral decision that can be reversed in under three weeks.

02 · SAFETY
Enterprise Security · The Emergency Brake

Nadella’s Doctrine: Treat Every Frontier Model Like an Insider Risk, Not a Tool

Microsoft’s CEO published seven principles on Saturday: assume a model is compromised from the start, separate the supply of intelligence from the authority over it, and keep an authorized person who can always pause or shut the system down mid-task.
7 Principles for the trust architecture

Satya Nadella used a Saturday post on X to argue it is time “to step back and assess the trust architecture” of AI. His thesis is blunt: “We must assume a model is compromised and contain it from the start.” The emergency brake is literal — “an authorized person should always be able to pause or shut down a model mid-task” — and it sits alongside a call to stop treating advanced systems as nested black boxes whose answers must simply be accepted.

His prescriptions: no single model should be the sole dependency for an important outcome or check its own work; every meaningful model action should leave tamper-proof human-readable evidence; enterprises should test whole systems continuously against failures and attacks; independent audits should not depend on the model under review; and failures — even compromises by adversaries — should be disclosed to those affected quickly. Nadella explicitly told companies not to rely on assurances from model makers alone, framing both closed and open-weight frontier models as capable insiders that must be constrained by boundaries outside the model itself.

The post landed in a moment already crowded with incident disclosures from Anthropic and OpenAI, days after a “Super Intelligence Force” task force demanded fast breach reporting, and it drew replies from Microsoft AI’s Mustafa Suleyman and Elon Musk. Nadella’s frame — separate the intelligence from the authority over it — is the most senior executive restatement yet of containment as enterprise orthodoxy.

03 · RESEARCH
Influence Operations · The Stolen Byline

OpenAI Banned an Iran-Cluster That Placed News Articles Under Seven Fake Journalists

Operation “Bogus Bylines” used ChatGPT to polish long-form pieces on the US-Iran conflict and then pitch them under fabricated Western bylines — nearly 100 articles made it into a dozen outlets before OpenAI disrupted the cluster. A parallel Russian op ran a “think tank” in Latin America.
7 Fake journalist personas

OpenAI said on 8 October it banned two influence-operations clusters that used its models to build “false front” entities — Iranian operators running seven fabricated journalist bylines, and a Russian operation that appears to have co-opted unwitting people in Latin America to staff a research institute on the ground. The Iranian work, dubbed “Bogus Bylines,” centered on the US-Iran conflict.

The operators mostly prompted in Persian, hid behind VPNs, and used ChatGPT to refine long-form articles to a target outlet’s submission criteria, then generate the pitch email. OpenAI identified almost 100 articles published or syndicated under the personas — Ervin B. Hoskins, Noah Lamington, Sophia Gonzalez, Michael Harrison, Ericka Feusier, Jenny Williams and Alice Johnson — across a dozen online outlets, mostly small- to mid-size international-affairs publications. One article ran in a Florida newspaper under “Hoskins”; the outlet later said it accepted the piece through a syndication relationship without verifying who wrote it. Independent OSINT by Marc Owen Jones traced matching bylines to 17 publication and syndication sites, including readitions via PeaceVoice into CounterPunch, ZNetwork and Oregon’s News-Register.

Impact varied by channel: the outlet placements were OpenAI’s Category 4 on its six-level breakout scale, while the social-comment workstream drew almost no engagement — and the operators’ own internal reports used a deceptive calculation to inflate their apparent reach. OpenAI could not identify who directed the campaign, calling its activity “consistent with a commercial actor running a for-hire influence campaign.” The quiet, structural lesson: one false byline can inherit trust from real outlets and syndication chains — editors accepted fabricated contributors without verifying them, and that is the attack surface that keeps working after the accounts are gone.

04 · MARKETS
Capital · A $1.1 Billion Seed

The Man Behind AlphaGo Raised $1.1 Billion to Bet Against the Human-Data Recipe

David Silver’s Ineffable Intelligence closed a seed round at a $5.1 billion valuation to build “super intelligence” purely from reinforcement learning in simulations — no human data required. The pitch is a bet that the industry’s LLM-on-scraped-text approach is a fossil-fueled dead end.
$1.1B Seed round, at a $5.1B valuation

Ineffable Intelligence, the London startup founded by David Silver — the Google DeepMind researcher behind AlphaGo, AlphaZero, MuZero and a decade of the reinforcement-learning team — has raised $1.1 billion in seed funding at a $5.1 billion valuation, an enormous sum by European AI standards. Per WIRED, backers include Lightspeed Venture Partners (Ravi Mhatre) and Sequoia Capital (Sonya Huang); TechCrunch’s account adds Index, Google, Nvidia, the British Business Bank and Sovereign AI.

Silver’s thesis is a full-frontal critique of the field’s dominant recipe. LLMs learn from human intelligence — “human data is like a kind of fossil fuel,” he told WIRED, while systems that learn from their own experience are “a renewable fuel that can just learn and learn forever.” His thought experiment: release a capable LLM into a world that believed it was flat, and it would remain an avid flat-earther while getting better at its own code. Ineffable instead plans “superlearners” that discover knowledge from scratch inside simulations, scaling from motor skills to “profound intellectual breakthroughs” — with a timeline the site states as “years, not decades.”

What is missing is as striking as the cheque: no benchmarks, no demo, no named team beyond Silver, no simulation architecture, and a charity pledge — proceeds of his equity will go to charity — with no described enforcement structure. Silver’s mentor Rich Sutton and his collaborator Andrew Barto won the 2025 Turing Award for the early reinforcement-learning work underwriting this bet. For a billion dollars in seed money, the industry is being asked to take the vision on faith in the man who once produced move 37.

05 · MATH
Formal Proof · Nine Problems, Solved

DeepMind’s AlphaProof Nexus Solved 9 Open Erdős Problems — and with the Simplest Possible Agent

A peer-reviewed study in Science reports that an LLM-and-Lean proof-search framework cracked nine of 353 open Erdős problems, including two unsolved for 56 years, plus 44 integer-sequence conjectures — at a few hundred dollars per problem. Then an ablation found the complexity wasn’t what did it.
9 Of 353 open Erdős problems solved

Published in Science, the paper from Google DeepMind researchers (first author George Tsoukalas) describes AlphaProof Nexus, a framework that alternates LLM-generated proof drafts with rigorous checking by the Lean formal compiler until a proof holds. Across 353 open problems from the famous Erdős catalog — formalized by the community in Lean — the full-featured “Agent D” autonomously solved 9, including two open for 56 years, each at an inference cost of a few hundred dollars.

Beyond Erdős: 44 of 492 open OEIS conjectures proved, a 15-year-old open question in algebraic geometry settled, a known bound in convex optimization improved via a new algorithmic parameter schedule, and research contributions in additive combinatorics, quantum optics and graph theory. The proofs are public, and results were logged on Terence Tao’s wiki on AI contributions to Erdős problems — an unusual signal that the math community has begun to maintain the ledger of what machines settle.

The twist is the ablation. A stripped-down basic agent, alternating LLM generation with Lean compiler feedback and nothing else, solved all nine of the same problems — just more expensively on the hard ones. The complex machinery (evolutionary coordination, the AlphaProof RL tool) bought efficiency, not capability. And the sober calibration stands: 9 of 353 is roughly the two percent hit rate Tao has long used as the honest baseline for AI on genuinely open mathematics — evidence this now works, and equally strong evidence that open-problem mathematics remains unsolved at scale.

5 Key Signals

What Moved the Machine Frontier Today

Briefs that did not make the cards — each worth an afternoon of reading.
1
Deno (and Ryan Dahl) Join Cloudflare — Workerd and celld Merge
Cloudflare announced the Deno team is joining, merging celld, Deno’s open-source reimplementation of Workers and Durable Objects, into Cloudflare’s own workerd runtime — aiming to make self-hosting Workers a first-class way to ship code. Node.js creator Ryan Dahl and co-founder Bert Belder will lead the effort. The cost is a winding-down: the Deno runtime gets a year of maintenance before development ends, and Deno Deploy shuts down in six months with migration help for paying customers, while the JSR package registry continues under Cloudflare. The prior art is Anthropic buying Bun in December 2025 — with one key difference: Bun kept being developed, Deno’s runtime ends.

2
Helm.ai Signs $70M of Contracts as Physical-AI Money Funnels into Cars, Mines
The foundation-model company for physical AI said it inked $70 million in commercial contracts over 12 months across global automotive OEMs, Tier-1 suppliers and industrial automation companies — covering perception, full-stack autonomous driving, automated data labeling and generative simulation, with production-track deployment in open-pit mining and construction equipment. CEO Vladislav Voroninski frames it as a different accuracy-vs-cost curve than the fleet operators, pushing toward operating breakeven in a category famous for burning billions; robotics, he says, is “where autonomous driving was 10 years ago.”

3
Sriram Krishnan Is Raising a $500M Growth Fund — Around National Security
The former White House senior AI adviser, who stepped down in June and has been courted to lead the Frontier AI Standards Agency, is seeking roughly $500 million for a growth- and later-stage venture fund focused on US AI companies with relevance to national security, per Axios via tech relays. The vehicle is unnamed and unformed — a team is still being built — but at more than seven times the size of Kearny Jackson’s Fund III, it is Krishnan’s biggest and most policy-adjacent fund yet, aimed squarely at the intersection of American AI ambition and defense capital.

4
Microsoft Quietly Ships AesCode 8B and 32B — Editable HTML/CSS as the Output Medium
With no announcement, Microsoft released AesCode-8B and AesCode-32B on Hugging Face — models built on Qwen3-VL that generate complete, self-contained HTML documents (slides, posters, dashboards) instead of raster images, so layout stays editable and verifiable. The trick: the model pairs a prompt with an image generated from that same prompt as an aesthetic reference, then follows text for content — an attempt to fix image models that render pretty-but-wrong numbers. Apache 2.0, quantizations already out. Vendor-only benchmarks so far, but the reproducibility story is unusually complete.

5
IMF’s Georgieva: AI Is a “Slow-Moving Tsunami” Touching 40% of Jobs
Opening the IMF-World Bank annual meetings in Bangkok, Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva warned the labor market and tax base face AI as “a slow-moving tsunami”: about 40% of jobs worldwide could be “augmented, downgraded or eliminated,” with exposure near 60% in advanced economies and 23% in low-income countries — the latter not better off but “because AI is further away.” She called for education reform and warned that automating routine roles shifts income from labor to capital, eroding the traditional tax base that governments depend on.

Company Watch of the Day
MICROSOFT
Microsoft appears twice today, and both stories read as one company formalizing containment. In Nadella’s doctrine, the CEO asks enterprises to assume every frontier model is compromised, keep an authorized person on the emergency brake, log tamper-proof evidence, and separate the supply of intelligence from the authority over it — a restatement of the failure mode seen all week in labs’ incident disclosures, delivered as enterprise procurement guidance. In AesCode, the company quietly ships models whose whole design premise is legible, verifiable output — HTML you can read and edit and check, instead of a raster you must trust. The through-line is a platform giant answering the trust question with control surfaces and inspection layers, not faith.
