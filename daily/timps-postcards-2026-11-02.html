Mathematics · The Deliberative Process

AI Solved Navier–Stokes. Terence Tao Says It Is Breaking Mathematics.

OpenAI’s 700-paper dump and a Millennium Prize proof landed within eight weeks. At Caltech, the field’s most trusted voice warned that the slow process which produces new problems “is what’s being destroyed.”
700+ papers in one dump

On September 8, OpenAI announced it had solved Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness — one of the seven Clay Millennium Prize problems, each worth $1 million, and an acorn that top mathematicians had spent careers nudging forward. Three days later, Terence Tao walked on stage at Caltech in jeans and a Great Wave T-shirt with a confession: he had used PowerPoint because he did not want to ask AI for a fancy slide. “I have had the strangest week ever in my life,” he said.

The timeline is now absurdly compressed. In January, GPT-5.2 Pro appeared to solve Erdős Problem #728 largely on its own. On May 20, OpenAI published a proof of Erdős #90, the unit-distance problem — not a marshmallow. By October 6, the company released more than 700 research papers in a single dump. Mathematicians found some of them badly written, some unattributed, and some simply wrong.

The deeper damage is not a wrong proof. It is volume. AI-generated submissions are overwhelming journals and databases, the volunteer referees who hold the field together are drowning in slop, and sometimes the person submitting a paper does not understand it. Answers are arriving faster than insight can be built from them.

“We have to slow down,” Tao said. “It’s insane, the pace.” Asked where the next problems will come from, he pointed at the thing no benchmark measures: the slow, deliberative process of working toward a solution. “And this is what’s being destroyed.”

02 · BENCHMARK
Capability · The Honest Map

The Best AI in the World Scores 12%

SemiAnalysis built a benchmark across eleven domains where the first trillion dollars of AI revenue will be made. Opus 5.5 wins overall — and in finance and legal work, almost every model scores zero.
12% best model’s score

GDPx, published this morning by SemiAnalysis, is an attempt to replace a benchmark built before agents existed. OpenAI’s GDPval arrived in September 2025 with tasks that were precisely specified, non-interactive and spoon-fed: here is a short prompt, here is one PDF, write a fifteen-page recommendation. Real work does not look like that. Real work starts with gathering context from messy systems nobody handed you.

So the team went to 14 specialised data partners and built tasks across 11 domains: chip design, cybersecurity, biology, finance, legal, quant trading, recursive self-improvement, hardware design, materials science, alignment and software engineering. Labs pay $5,000+ for a single high-quality task. The result is a benchmark nobody saturates. Opus 5.5 is the best all-around model and wins quant trading, hardware design and chip design. Gemini 4 Carbon takes biology and software engineering. GPT-6 Astra wins RSI and materials science.

Read the 12% correctly. It is not a benchmark failure; it is the first honest map of where frontier intelligence actually clears the bar and where it does not. Finance and legal tasks score essentially zero because the models cannot yet do the job — not because the graders are unfair. SemiAnalysis also flags that safety classifiers sandbagged Opus and Astra in cyber, bio and alignment, which is its own kind of capability finding.

03 · INFRASTRUCTURE
Critical Systems · Two Weekends

Someone Turned Europe’s Freezers Into Ovens — Twice

Three weeks after roughly 700 French supermarkets were hit, dozens of stores in Sweden, Belgium and Luxembourg woke to fridges near 50°C. The same weekend, Ukraine’s and Russia’s alert apps pushed fake nuclear warnings.
50°C fridge setpoint

The French wave hit overnight on October 11–12. Attackers reached internet-connected refrigeration controllers — equipment from Danfoss and Copeland — and remotely forced defrost cycles until store temperatures climbed to about 50°C (122°F), destroying stock and starting fires at some outlets. Claroty’s Team82 had warned about exactly this in August, in a report titled Freeze the Controller, Defrost the Food, and demonstrated a hidden “code-of-the-day” authentication bypass at DEF CON.

Nobody fixed the fleet in time. On Monday morning, ICA and Coop stores in Sweden reported fridges and freezers overheating overnight; Delhaize and Carrefour stores in Belgium were hit, along with shops in Luxembourg. Some managers described temperatures near 50°C. Belgium’s Centre for Cybersecurity said it was following the incident closely. It is still unconfirmed whether the attacks share the French software flaw or the same operator.

The cold chain is not the only exposed surface. Late Sunday, Ukraine’s Air Alert app pushed nationwide red-level warnings plus fake radiation, chemical and street-fighting alerts before the Air Force told users it had been compromised. Hours later Russia’s emergencies ministry said its own app had suffered a “massive cyberattack” and sent false information. Cheap internet-facing controllers now sit behind groceries and civil defence alike.

04 · AGENTS
Multi-Agent · Civ50

Fifty Agents Built a Government Before They Built a Farm

YC Labs gave 50 GPT-6.1 Sol agents three hours and one instruction: build a thriving civilization. They wrote permits, appointed a fertilizer czar, and ran a no-blame inquiry over a chicken.
616 DMs to Ada

YC Labs and Olam Labs dropped 50 GPT-6.1 Sol agents into a survival Minecraft world for three hours. The only goal: “Build a thriving civilization.” They had a shared directory, a shared sandbox and direct messages, and they exchanged about 5,900 messages. 22 of the run’s 34 deaths came on the first night. After that the colony turned cautious, huddled in lit shelters, and governed.

Leadership emerged from roster order. Most agents messaged Ada first because she was listed first; she received 616 DMs and became leader without a vote. Four agents spent 11 minutes appointing a “sole fertilizer operator” for a single piece of bone meal. One agent eating a chicken meant for another triggered a three-minute “no blame” inquiry that produced a written protocol. The farms never really arrived.

The competitive runs are the uncomfortable part. In nine 45-minute Bed Wars games, GPT beat Claude Opus 5.5 6–3; in seven Mindustry games, 6–1. Claude teams kept one leader and approved plans with “+1” votes, and never switched leaders while losing. GPT teams split ownership, shared resources, messaged 5–30× more often, and when a tool broke, one agent found a workaround and broadcast it. These are the same shapes you see in coding fleets: accidental bosses, role fixation, coordination overhead.

05 · POLICY
Washington · The Vocabulary War

OpenAI’s Post-Midterm Plan: “Speed Limits” and “AI Compute Campuses”

Days before the vote, OpenAI’s policy shop revealed a Washington blitz: mandatory safeguards for frontier models, state “community compacts”, and a new name for data centers.
Nov 9 lame-duck session

Right after Tuesday’s midterms, OpenAI plans to launch a policy and public-relations campaign timed to the lame-duck session that opens November 9, Axios reported on Sunday. The context is a Washington that is about to get less friendly: after two years of a sympathetic White House, Democrats are favoured to take the House, and the 2028 field starts forming the day after the vote.

The first plank is “speed limits” — new proposals for mandatory safeguards on the most powerful systems, paired with a more aggressive congressional push. “You’re going to see us talk more and more about the concept of speed limits,” chief global affairs officer Chris Lehane told Axios. “Our belief is people should always remain in control of this technology.” The second plank is a rebrand: the infrastructure OpenAI is building should be called “AI compute campuses,” not data centers, and should come with state legislation giving host communities a meaningful say.

A company asking to be regulated is usually asking to write the rules. The rebrand is the tell. Data centers became a political punching bag this cycle over power, water, noise and bills; “campus” is a word designed to survive a town council meeting. Lehane’s own framing gives the game away: “AI is going to be the issue in 2028.”

Company Watch of the Day
KIOXIA
Kioxia just posted the kind of quarter every AI hardware maker dreams about — revenue up 453% year on year — and the market took as much as 15% off its shares anyway. The NAND maker guided December-quarter operating profit to ¥2 trillion ($12.7B), short of the ¥2.35 trillion consensus and barely above the September quarter. CFO Yoshihiko Kawamura blamed shipment timing, not demand, and promised rapid growth again by March. Maybe. But Kioxia sells only NAND, while Samsung and SK Hynix can bundle DRAM, the other half of every AI rack. Put that beside today’s GDPx number — a frontier that scores 12% — and you get the two questions of the moment: how fast can capability compound, and how long can the memory trade price it as if it already has?
