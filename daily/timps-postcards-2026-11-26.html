Health · The Clinical Leap

Neuralink Clears the First US Pivotal Brain-Implant Trial

The FDA has opened a pivotal study of the implant in adults with severe paralysis from ALS. More than 30 people have already used earlier versions in three countries.
>30 implanted participants in earlier feasibility studies in the US, Canada and the UK, with thousands of hours of real-world use

Neuralink announced on Wednesday that the US Food and Drug Administration had approved the LINK Pivotal Study, which it calls the first pivotal trial for an implantable brain-computer interface in the United States. The study will evaluate the safety and effectiveness of the Neuralink BCI system in people with severe paralysis caused by amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS). Enrolment is open, and the company says it will add sites over time. Participants will use the implant to control digital devices by thought, with the trial tracking functional outcomes that improve independence in daily life.

The approval rests on earlier feasibility studies in the United States, Canada and the United Kingdom, where Neuralink says more than 30 implanted participants have contributed thousands of hours of real-world use. It lists higher education, creative work, video games and communicating with family among the things participants have done with the system. The pivotal study will also follow long-term safety and reliability across multiple clinical sites — the part that matters if a device is ever to move from trial to market.

“Pivotal” is a regulatory word, not a claim that the device works. It is the study design regulators expect before a company can ask to market a product. Neuralink’s announcement did not publish a participant count, a primary endpoint or a completion date. Bloomberg and other outlets corroborated the approval; the details available today come from the company.

This is the moment a brain implant stops being a demo and becomes a clinical programme. The engineering story is now the easy part: the hard part is showing, across enough patients and enough years, that a coin-sized implant is safe and that thought-controlled clicks translate into something a person can actually use. Watch the trial registry, the endpoint choices and the adverse-event reporting. A first pivotal trial is a hinge, not a finish line.

Caveats. The announcement is from Neuralink; the FDA has approved a study, not the device itself. The “first pivotal trial” framing is the company’s. Participant numbers and hours of use are self-reported, and no peer-reviewed results are attached. Watch for the trial’s registration details, enrolment pace and any update on the earlier feasibility cohorts.

Markets · The Leverage Lesson

The AI Prophet Who Called It Lost 65% in a Quarter

Leopold Aschenbrenner’s Situational Awareness nearly died in July. Its third-quarter letter puts the damage at 65% after fees — and still claims an 88.5% gain for the year.
−65% net-of-fee return in the third quarter, against +88.5% year to date through September, according to the investor letter

The Wall Street Journal reported that Situational Awareness told investors it lost 65% after fees in the third quarter, as the AI-focused fund worked through the wreckage of a leveraged summer. The fund, run by former OpenAI researcher Leopold Aschenbrenner, came close to permanent impairment in July when borrowing-fuelled bets on AI names soured. It is still up 88.5% after fees for the year through September.

The letter concedes the error. “Many of our predictions—which seemed like science fiction two years ago—came true. And yet we also learned how wrong we could be, and how merciless the market is when we are,” Aschenbrenner wrote, according to the Journal. His new rule: “Volatility, yes; existential risk, even if we are totally wrong, never.” He says the portfolio has been restructured for resilience and that the fund has been investing without taking new money.

The scale of the round trip is visible in public filings. The fund’s reported 13F positions fell from $20.24 billion at the end of June to $1.58 billion at September 30 — a 92% reduction, with 21 of 26 lines closed. What remained was roughly two-thirds call options. Those filings cover US-listed positions only, not the whole book. Coverage of the July unwind put assets falling from about $45 billion to under $10 billion in less than a month.

The 88.5% headline is arithmetic, not vindication: a fund that roughly quintupled by June can lose two-thirds in three months and still be way up. The useful lesson is about structure. Being right about AI and wrong about leverage produced the same outcome as being wrong about AI — a forced sale into someone else’s profit. And the letter stops at September 30, before October cracked the trade again.

Caveats. The figures come from an investor letter reported by the Journal and relayed by secondary outlets; there is no independent audit. The 13F is a partial, lagged snapshot of US-listed holdings. The quarter ends before the October AI sell-off, so the fourth-quarter result is unknown. Watch the next letter, any new fund terms, and whether the options-heavy rebuild survives another drawdown.

03 · SECURITY
Cyber · The Machine-Speed Attack

Salt Typhoon Is Running an AI Pen-Testing Framework

Microsoft says Chinese and North Korean state groups have added ARTEX to their toolkits. It can go from reconnaissance to privileged access in minutes.
Minutes how quickly Microsoft says ARTEX can move from reconnaissance to persistent, privileged access inside a target organisation

Microsoft Threat Intelligence published a detailed warning on Wednesday about ARTEX, an AI-powered penetration-testing framework it says is being abused by state-sponsored and criminal hackers. It named six groups using the tool: Storm-0940, Storm-3358, Onyx Sleet, Pearl Sleet, Crystal Typhoon and Salt Typhoon. The last of those is the Chinese group blamed for breaching at least nine US telecommunications companies in 2024.

ARTEX was published on GitHub in July 2026 by a user called Autumn-27, with a Chinese-language interface and a Go backend that coordinates multiple language-model agents. A goals model breaks a target into verifiable objectives; a planner reads a knowledge graph of discovered systems and issues short “intents”; worker agents execute them with a real shell and offensive tools; a reporter writes up findings. Operators can plug in almost any model, including open-weight models stripped of guardrails, and a pool function switches providers when one runs out of quota or is rate-limited.

The original source was removed in October, but forks survived, and in November a Chinese security company, ThreatBook, claimed ownership and began selling commercial ARTEX Pro access. Microsoft stresses that the framework brings no new exploit techniques — it chains existing tools such as nmap with SQL injection, template injection, file uploads and credential attacks. The durable signature is behaviour: unusually broad bursts of requests and rapid retries from one source. The FBI issued its own advisory two weeks ago. The New York Times and NextGov reported that ARTEX was allegedly used in attacks on the CIA, the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence.

The threat is orchestration, not invention. A single operator can now run the whole intrusion loop at machine speed, and because the tool accepts any model, no provider’s safety filter reliably sees it. That makes attribution from malware samples weaker and behavioural detection more valuable. Watch whether the agencies confirm the reported breaches, and how quickly defenders adopt machine-speed response.

Caveats. The group names and technical findings are Microsoft’s, based on its own telemetry. Using ARTEX does not by itself identify an attacker. The reported attacks on US intelligence agencies come from the NYT and NextGov as cited by The Record, not from a confirmed agency statement. Watch Microsoft&rsquos detection guidance, the FBI advisory and any formal attribution.

04 · PRIVACY
Courts · The Data Auction

42 States Move to Block the Sale of 21 Million Character.AI Chats

The bankrupt chatbot company wants to auction user histories on December 1. Attorneys general say the buyer would inherit intimate conversations — and no obligations.
21M+ US accounts whose chat histories are in the proposed sale, including more than 2.1 million registered to minors

California Attorney General Rob Bonta and 41 counterparts, plus the District of Columbia, asked a Delaware bankruptcy court on Tuesday to reject Character.AI’s plan to sell its platform and the private chat histories of more than 21 million US accounts. More than 2.1 million of those accounts belong to minors. The auction is scheduled for December 1, 2026, and would transfer chats alongside account data, IP addresses, metadata and the company’s own risk scores.

The states say the sale includes conversations with characters presented as therapists and physicians, covering self-harm, eating disorders and suicidal thoughts, as well as explicit sexual exchanges involving children. Character.AI had told users “we do not sell personal information for monetary consideration.” Under the proposed terms, the buyer would not have to honour the privacy policy, delete data on request or refrain from reselling it.

The bidder is also a problem. The only one disclosed is Smirks and Pangs Acquisition LLC, incorporated in Delaware on November 13, one day before Character.AI chose it as the base bid. The contract names no parent or investors, permits foreign investors whose identities are shared only with the company, and would require hiring only Character.AI’s top lawyer. The states also want a $350,000 breakup fee rejected. A separate $20 million licence of Character.AI’s technology to Disney, which does not include user chats, still awaits final approval.

Bankruptcy is not a privacy eraser. If a company can sell the conversations it promised to protect simply by putting them in an asset pile, the promise was never worth much. The clean fix is simple: exclude minors’ chats and sensitive health data from any sale, and make buyer obligations a condition rather than a hope. Watch the December 1 hearing and whether a named, accountable buyer appears.

Caveats. The objections come from an attorney-general filing and press release; the court has not ruled. Coalition counts vary by statement because some include the District of Columbia. New chatbot safety laws in California, Colorado, New York and Connecticut take effect on January 1, 2027, but are not yet in force. Watch the court&rsquos decision, the final bidder list and any revised sale terms.

India · The Slow Comeback

Shein Returned to India With Reliance. It Has 6 Million Users.

Sensor Tower says the relaunched app is flat since January, against Myntra’s 100 million. The technology that made Shein fast did not travel with the licence.
6M average monthly active users in India in October, versus about 100 million for market leader Myntra

Bloomberg reports that Shein’s return to India through Reliance Retail is off to a slow start. The app averaged about 6 million monthly active users in October, down from September and broadly unchanged since January, according to Sensor Tower estimates. Myntra, an indirect Walmart unit, averages about 100 million. Shein relaunched in February 2025 after being among the Chinese apps banned by New Delhi in 2020. India accounted for 67% of its monthly users in 2019.

People familiar with the venture told Bloomberg that Reliance runs product selection, marketing and sales, while Shein has not shared the proprietary system that lets it spot, design and produce new styles in under 10 days. Reliance has had to build vendor onboarding and production workflows from scratch, using smaller Indian manufacturers unfamiliar with Shein’s Gen-Z styles. Training, waste and turnaround times all rose. Early leadership churn did not help: former Meta executive Manish Chopra, who was helping set up the venture, left in 2024.

Engagement is thin. Indian users spent about four minutes a week in the app over the last two months, against a global average of 6.6 minutes. September downloads fell 73% year over year to 790,000, even as advertising spend rose 60%; October slipped further to 740,000. The market Shein is re-entering is projected to reach $45 billion by 2028, but Myntra, Tata’s Trent, Aditya Birla Fashion and Meesho have spent the ban years filling the gap.

A licence can buy a brand name, not the machine behind it. Shein’s advantage was never the logo; it was a supply chain that compressed design-to-shelf into days. Reliance has scale, retail reach and Isha Ambani’s attention, but it is rebuilding the hard part with different suppliers in a market that no longer waits. Watch whether local lead times fall and whether the licence terms change.

Caveats. Bloomberg’s account relies on people familiar with private matters who asked not to be named; Shein and Reliance did not respond. Sensor Tower figures are estimates, not audited user counts. The $45 billion market figure is a projection. Watch the next quarterly engagement numbers and any shift from licence to deeper ownership.
