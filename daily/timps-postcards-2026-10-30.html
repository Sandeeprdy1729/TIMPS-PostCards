Crypto · Largest Hack Ever

Binance Investigates $6.3B Hack, Blames Compromised DevOps Laptop

TRM Labs attributes it to North Korea's TraderTraitor; Binance pledges to cover all losses in what could be the largest crypto theft ever.
$6.3B drained in minutes

Binance says a compromised DevOps laptop may have enabled hackers to drain roughly $6.3 billion from its wallets across 10 blockchains in about 20 minutes. TRM Labs attributes the attack to North Korea-based TraderTraitor. Tether froze over $1.5B in USDT and Circle froze over $800M in USDC in response.

Binance temporarily suspended deposits and withdrawals while investigating and working with law enforcement. The exchange has pledged to cover all losses. If confirmed as the largest crypto theft ever (surpassing Bybit's $1.5B hack in February 2025), it would mark a new scale of North Korean crypto heists.

Even the world's largest crypto exchange remains vulnerable to operational security failures. The speed and scale of the drain underscore how cross-chain theft can move funds before freezes take effect.

02 · CLOUD
Earnings · AI Demand

Amazon AWS Grows 39% as Spending Hits $220B Capex Target

AWS revenue beats at $45.98B with record operating margin, but Amazon's free cash flow turns deeply negative on AI infrastructure spend.
39% AWS growth

Amazon reported Q3 AWS revenue of $45.98 billion (up 39% YoY), beating expectations and hitting its fastest growth rate since Q4 2021. AWS operating income rose 55% to $17.77 billion. CEO Andy Jassy said AWS core compute and storage grew at their fastest rates in several years.

But the AI arms race is costly: Amazon spent over $77B on property and equipment in Q3, leading to free cash outflow of $42.4B. The company still expects to spend around $220B on capex in 2026. Prime Video is now profitable on its own with a revenue run rate in the tens of billions.

Hyperscalers are all showing the same pattern: AI demand is driving strong cloud growth, but the capex required to meet it is crushing free cash flow. Investors are learning to live with massive spending if growth stays strong.

PC Market · Weak Outlook

Intel Plunges 23% on Weak PC Demand and Bleak Outlook

Client computing falls 5% as memory prices soar; CFO says PC slowdown will continue through first half of next year.
-23% stock drop after hours

Intel reported Q3 revenue of $16.5 billion in line with expectations, but issued a weak Q4 forecast that sent shares down over 23% in after-hours trading. Revenue in its Client Computing and Physical AI group fell 5% YoY to $8.1 billion, missing estimates, while Data Center and AI revenue jumped 78% to $7.3 billion.

Intel expects Q4 adjusted EPS of 25 cents on revenue of $15B-$16B, well below analyst expectations of 43 cents and $17.1B. CFO David Zinsner said the PC slowdown will continue at least through the first half of next year, driven by soaring memory and component costs.

Intel's transformation is still uneven. While its AI/data center business is growing fast, the core PC business remains weak and is dragging down the overall outlook.

04 · PLATFORMS
Content · AI Scraping

Reddit Moves to Logged-In-Only Access to Fight AI Scraping

The platform plans to require login for full access in select markets, as DAUs miss expectations and revenue growth slows.
130M DAUq

Reddit plans to become a primarily logged-in experience to combat AI scraping, rolling out tests outside the US this quarter. The move comes as Reddit reported Q3 revenue of $895 million (up 53% YoY) but DAUq of 130.3 million fell below expectations. Q4 revenue guidance also came in below estimates, sending shares down over 17%.

Reddit has been aggressively monetizing its data through licensing deals, but AI companies scraping content without permission have become a major concern across the internet. Requiring login would give Reddit more control over access and better data for moderation and targeting.

The open web is closing. More platforms are realizing that freely accessible content is being harvested by AI labs without compensation, and they're taking action to protect it.

05 · MARKETS
Finance · New Products

NYSE Owner Plans US-Regulated Hyperliquid Perpetual Markets

ICE takes stake in Trade.xyz as traditional finance moves deeper into crypto derivatives.
NYSE entering crypto perps

Intercontinental Exchange (NYSE owner) plans to launch US-regulated perpetual futures markets based on Hyperliquid, working with market operator Trade.xyz in which ICE is taking a stake. The move brings one of crypto's most popular decentralized perpetual platforms into the regulated traditional finance ecosystem.

Meanwhile, Coinbase returned to profitability for the first time in a year with Q3 net income of $719 million despite revenue falling 35% to $1.22 billion. Polymarket briefly suspended trading after a security incident with a third-party provider before resuming.

The lines between TradFi and crypto continue to blur. When the NYSE wants to list Hyperliquid-style perpetuals, it's a sign the market structure is maturing whether regulators like it or not.

BINANCE
