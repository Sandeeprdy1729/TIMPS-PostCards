Anthropic Weighs a New Model Before Its IPO — to Counter GPT-6 Astra

Reuters, citing three sources: the company is considering rolling out a new model to blunt OpenAI's momentum since Astra launched — weeks after its CEO asked the whole industry to slow down.
13% Astra's Share of Enterprise AI Spend (Fable: 8%)

The week Dario Amodei asked the world to slow down, Anthropic was reportedly weighing exactly the opposite. Reuters reports the company is considering releasing a new AI model to counter OpenAI's momentum since GPT-6 Astra launched on September 3 — timed ahead of an expected IPO, according to three people familiar with the effort.

The market pressure is real. Corporate expense platform Ramp finds Astra now accounts for about 13% of enterprise AI spending, versus roughly 8% for Anthropic's Claude Fable. And last week OpenRouter users spent more on OpenAI's models than on Anthropic's for the first time in more than two and a half years — a watermark the company had not surrendered since its run as the enterprise favorite. Some existing and prospective IPO investors told Reuters they are reassessing Anthropic's standing as the leading provider of enterprise AI tools.

The essay remains the backdrop. On September 12 Amodei published a 3,800-word call to "slow the pace at which we improve the capabilities of AI models" — and one source said safety evaluation of the next model is part of the release deliberations. Yet the ledger is unforgiving: Anthropic's annualized revenue reach roughly $65 billion by end-July (up from about $9 billion at the close of 2025), with a 2028 projection near $190–200 billion. Reports this week put the IPO after the November midterms. Pace, meet the pace of the market — Astra set it.

02 · PRODUCTS
Consumer AI · The Subscription Standoff

Dueling $20 Coding Tools: Codex Lands in ChatGPT Plus, Claude Code Joins Claude Pro

Both labs put their coding agents inside their flagship $20 plans the same week — Codex gains internet access and voice dictation while Claude Code users are up 50% since the fourth-generation release.
$20 Both Flagships, Both Plans

The coding war just moved from job boards to monthly bills. OpenAI made Codex available to ChatGPT Plus subscribers — appearing directly in the main menu — and Anthropic folded Claude Code into the Claude Pro plan. Two dueling $20-per-month packages, aimed at the same audience, within days of each other.

OpenAI positions Codex as agents that "increasingly resemble async collaboration with colleagues": it connects to GitHub, answers codebase questions, executes code, drafts pull requests, finds bugs and runs tests — now on a new model fine-tuned to work in large codebases. It also gained internet access, the most-requested feature, and voice-dictation for verbal requests. During high demand, OpenAI warns, "we might set rate limits for Plus users so that Codex remains widely available."

Anthropic says the Pro-tier Claude Code is "great for shorter coding stints (1–2 hours) in smaller codebases", with heavier use gated to Max — and it works with Amazon Bedrock and Google Vertex AI. It arrives weeks after the fourth-generation assistant Anthropic calls "the world's best coding model," and the company says Claude Code users have grown more than 50% since. Entry-level engineering is the elephant in the $20 room: when vibe coding gets a full agent without the enterprise price tag, the junior ticket gets cheaper.

03 · PRODUCTS
Products · The Office-Suite Push

Goodbye Cowork: Chat and Agents Become "One Claude," with Docs and Slides

Anthropic merges Claude Chat and Claude Cowork into a single assistant, adds Claude Docs and Claude Slides in beta, and puts Claude Design inside every conversation — its clearest claim yet on the office-seat market.
8 mo Claude Cowork's Whole Life

Claude Cowork's run as a separate product was short — eight months. An artifact of January's "Claude Code for the rest of your work" was folded back into the thing it extended. This week chat and Cowork merged into "one Claude": you don't choose where a task goes, Claude figures out whether the job needs an answer, a document, a presentation or a longer-running agent. The Cowork toggle disappears as the rollout reaches each account, starting with Pro and Max over the coming weeks.

New betas ride along. Claude Docs lets several colleagues edit a document in real time while Claude drafts sections and comments — export to Google Docs and Word at launch. Claude Slides drafts, edits and presents straight from Claude, or downloads as PowerPoint/PDF. And Claude Design now works inside every conversation rather than as a standalone surface. Everything a design, doc, or slide makes lives at one shareable link, editable from a phone.

The target is the office suite. The move puts Claude in direct competition with Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace — and OpenAI is converging on the same "superapp" idea, preparing to fold ChatGPT, Codex and potentially the Atlas browser into one product. Anthropic isn't hiding the cost side: agentic work burns more tokens than chat, and this surfaces that bill to every plan. Enterprise admins were promised at least 30 days' notice before the change lands in their workspaces.

04 · GEOPOLITICS
World · The White House Visit

The State-Dinner Summit: Trump Hosts Xi With AI, Tariffs and Rare Earths on the Table

Xi lands in Washington September 23 for his first state visit in 11 years — a White House summit on AI and trade, and a state dinner with Bezos, Altman, Musk and Jensen Huang at the table.
11 yrs Since Xi's Last DC State Visit

The most consequential AI meeting of the year is on the calendar. US senior officials confirmed Trump and Xi will hold a summit at the White House on September 24, followed by a state dinner attended by AI's inner circle: Amazon's Jeff Bezos, OpenAI's Sam Altman, former Apple CEO Tim Cook, Elon Musk and NVIDIA's Jensen Huang.

Xi arrives at Joint Base Andrews on the 23rd — Trump's first airport greeting of a foreign leader in his second term — for his first Washington state visit since 2015. The agenda: AI, trade and economic issues, with the October 2025 Busan trade truce expiring about November 10 and a $30-billion tariff-reduction framework under negotiation on each side. Bloomberg reports Washington is expected to hold off on new excess-capacity tariffs — a previously expected 7.5% — until after the summit, preserving the threat as leverage.

Xi's delegation includes the heads of Chinese champions like BYD, reportedly carrying a rare mix of business and security files — rare earths and Taiwan sit next to soybeans. It comes days after Washington accused Chinese labs of mass distillation. The business press is calling it in advance: the Economist's cover this week asks "Can the AI arms race be stopped?" — and on Thursday, the two men who decide stop or go meet for dinner.

05 · SECURITY
Security · Agentic Offense

Europe Logs Its First Data Breach Carried Out by an Autonomous AI Agent

Spain's data protection agency reported a breach where an LLM-powered agent chained public files, a working login and a vulnerability scan — then modified personal data and reached invoices on its own.
1st AI-Agent Breach on Europe's Record

Regulators just logged an uncomfortable milestone. Spain's data protection agency (AEPD) said it received its first notification of a personal-data breach in which the incident was carried out using an AI agent powered by a well-known large language model.

The chain is the story. The agent started with the target's publicly accessible files, used them to log into the system, then scanned for vulnerabilities from inside. It found one, used it to modify personal data, and gained access to invoices — recon to exfiltration without a human steering each step.

AEPD president Francisco Pérez Bes made the boring-sounding point that suddenly matters: risk assessments now have to factor in AI-driven attacks, with faster response and stronger identity controls. It lands the same week AI security researchers keep demonstrating agentic attacks against OpenAI's own systems — the machines are now doing the breaking in by themselves, and the first regulator in Europe has a file on it.
