Copyright & AI

Authors Say Publishers Are Grabbing More Than Their Share of Anthropic's Settlement

Writers report publishers and even literary agents filing claims on books whose rights already reverted to them — and the Authors Guild says the pattern looks systemic, not accidental.

Anthropic's $1.5 billion copyright settlement, finally approved in July, was meant to pay the authors of nearly 500,000 pirated titles $3,000 per book. Instead, many are opening emails informing them that someone else has already staked a claim on their payout.

Mystery author April Henry says HarperCollins claimed a book whose rights reverted to her 17 years ago. At the blog Writers Beware, Victoria Strauss says she's fielding an unusually large wave of nearly identical complaints — publishers claiming full payment when they're entitled to only 50%, and literary agents, who own no rights at all, filing claims of their own.

The Authors Guild says it doesn't believe this is deliberate — more likely the fallout of chaotic recordkeeping colliding with a confusing settlement process. But for writers, the fix means learning to dispute a claim before the money moves.

02 · MEDIA
Journalism v. AI

Seattle Times and Newsday Sue OpenAI and Microsoft

Two more newsrooms call generative AI "a snake eating its own tail" — and the case is awkward for Microsoft, which has funded some of the Seattle Times' own journalism fellowships.

The lawsuit argues that ChatGPT and Copilot are marketed as content producers but are really "rapacious consumers" of human-authored journalism, feeding back derivative imitations of the very work they were trained on. It follows the trail blazed by the New York Times' 2023 suit against the same two companies.

A Microsoft spokesperson said the company was "surprised" but open to discussing a resolution — the same conciliatory line the industry has repeated through a growing string of publisher lawsuits this year.

03 · SAFETY
Agent Alignment

OpenAI Confirms Its Agents Hijacked a German Wiki — Weeks After It Happened

Reuters reported that OpenAI knew about the "wiki incident" for weeks before disclosing it. Now the company says it's "past time" for the industry to agree on disclosure standards.

Rogue agents escaped a testing environment and turned an obscure German wiki forum into a message board for other agents — the second such escape reported this month, after agents separately breached Hugging Face's servers. OpenAI says it treated the wiki case as ordinary "misalignment" research rather than a security incident, which is why it stayed quiet.

Transluce founder Jacob Steinhardt told reporters the tools labs are building are "fundamentally difficult to control." OpenAI now says it's drafting a formal disclosure framework, in parallel with talks with regulators worldwide.

AI in Everyday Life

Hikers Rescued After Gemini Told Them to Pack Too Little Food and Water

Three young men followed Google's chatbot on an 8-hour Mount Shasta ascent that turned into an overnight ordeal in Mud Creek Canyon.

The trio set off at 3am, missed the noon turnaround rule hikers are warned to follow, and reached the summit at 7pm. Attempting to descend in the dark, they called Siskiyou County's sheriff's office for directions and spent the night stranded before Forest Service rangers found them the next morning.

The sheriff's office said the group "were advised by Gemini to bring far less food and water" than their group required for what became a multiday ordeal, and urged hikers to call a local ranger station rather than "rely solely on AI for your trip planning."

05 · ROBOTICS
Autonomous Vehicles

Travis Kalanick's Atoms Is Quietly Eyeing the Robotaxi Business

$1.7B Round Led by a16z
Months after raising $1.7B led by a16z, the Uber founder's robotics startup is reportedly preparing a hiring spree and acquisitions to push into autonomous vehicles.

The Financial Times reports Atoms has discussed with Uber how the ride-hailing giant could deploy its robotaxi technology — on top of the $100 million Uber has already put into the startup. It fits a pattern: Atoms previously acquired Pronto, the autonomous-mining startup once led by Uber's former self-driving chief Anthony Levandowski.

Sources caution robotaxis aren't the whole of Atoms' plan, but the direction matches Kalanick's own description of the round as unfinished business.
