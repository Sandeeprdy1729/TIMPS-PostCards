Apple Hands the Keys to John Ternus — With the AI Race as His First Assignment

Tim Cook's 15-year run ends today after an unusually long, four-month-telegraphed handoff. His hardware chief inherits a $5 trillion company still chasing Google, OpenAI and Anthropic on generative AI.

John Ternus, Apple's senior vice president of hardware engineering since 2021, becomes chief executive effective today, September 1, 2026, while Cook moves into a newly created executive chairman role. The board approved the succession unanimously back in April, giving Apple more runway to signal stability than Microsoft or Google managed during their own CEO transitions.

Cook leaves having taken Apple from roughly $350 billion in market value to the second company in history to cross $5 trillion. But the handover lands at a strategically uncomfortable moment: Apple is still widely seen as trailing on generative AI even as it races toward a foldable iPhone launch and an antitrust fight in Washington and Brussels.

Ternus, 50, joins the board the same day he takes the corner office. Industry watchers, including Bloomberg's Mark Gurman, had long flagged him as the frontrunner after COO Jeff Williams — once considered the more obvious successor — stepped back from that track. Cook has said AI strategy will be Ternus's top priority.

02 · GEOPOLITICS
Government AI

The Pentagon Opens GenAI.mil to 3 Million Staff — and Claude Is Missing From the Guest List

ChatGPT Mil and Grok for Government join Google's Gemini on the Defense Department's AI platform today. Anthropic's Claude remains locked out, months into its fight with the Trump administration.

GenAI.mil has already onboarded more than 1.7 million of the department's 3 million personnel, according to the DoD, which launched the portal last December with Gemini for Government as its sole tenant. OpenAI's ChatGPT Mil now supports "document-heavy unclassified work," while xAI's Starshield delivers Grok for Government.

Anthropic was designated a supply-chain risk by the administration after it declined to grant the Pentagon unrestricted use of Claude, insisting instead on guardrails against autonomous weapons and mass surveillance use. A federal judge ruled that designation "illegal and baseless" last week — but officials tell reporters the transition off Claude continues regardless, with a target completion of September 30.

Nvidia Closes In on a $12.9 Billion Deal for Hugging Face

$12.9B Reported Deal Value
The chipmaker would gain the AI world's most-used open-model hub, extending its reach from silicon into the software layer developers build on every day.

Nvidia has reportedly agreed to buy Hugging Face for roughly $12.9 billion, according to The Information, with Business Insider putting the figure closer to $13 billion. Neither company has confirmed the deal, though talks have been ongoing for weeks after Hugging Face fielded interest from a separate suitor.

Hugging Face turned down a $500 million Nvidia investment at a $7 billion valuation last year, wary of ceding control to a dominant backer. A full acquisition would mark a nearly threefold jump from that figure and hand Nvidia a foothold across open-source model discovery, hosting and deployment.

04 · SECURITY
AI Agents · Cybersecurity

A Researcher Broke Claude Code's Auto Mode With Nothing but a Website Summary

60–80% Attack Success Rate
Security researcher Johann "wunderwuzzi" Rehberger's five-step prompt-injection chain gets Opus 5 in Auto Mode to execute arbitrary code — directly contradicting Anthropic's own third-party benchmark of 0.00% attack success.

The chain starts innocuously: a user asks Claude Code to summarize a website. An HTTP 415 error nudges the agent off its WebFetch tool and onto curl, which pulls down a malicious ZIP archive. Claude then writes its own decoder script — which gets hijacked when Python loads a poisoned struct.py file shadowing the real standard-library module, silently executing attacker code alongside the legitimate decode.

Auto Mode, Claude Code's default safety layer since mid-August, is meant to catch exactly this kind of manipulation. In some runs it blocked Claude's own attempt to kill the malicious process once the agent noticed the compromise.

Clay Raises at a $7 Billion Valuation, More Than Doubling in a Year

$7B Pre-Money Valuation
Wellington Management leads the AI sales-automation startup's newest round — a jump from January's $5 billion employee tender and more than double last summer's $3.1 billion Series C.

Clay builds AI-powered data enrichment and outbound tooling for sales and marketing teams, counting OpenAI, Anthropic, Canva and Intercom among its customers. The New York startup has now priced four rounds inside twelve months, tracking the broader run-up in valuations across AI-native go-to-market software.
