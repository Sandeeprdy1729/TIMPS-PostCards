Anthropic, OpenAI and Google Have Been Quietly Building Their Own AI Police Since July

Months before Dario Amodei's public plea to "pace the frontier," his lab was already meeting privately with its two biggest rivals to design a shared testing-and-auditing body — a bet that Washington won't move first.
3 Labs at the Table

The Information reported Sunday that Anthropic, OpenAI and Google DeepMind have held working-group meetings since July — at the executive level, below CEO — to design an industry-led standards body for technical testing and pre-release auditing of frontier models. Dario Amodei is said to be driving the effort, with Sam Altman backing it as a fallback for labs that don't want to wait on federal action.

The private track predates the weekend's public choreography. It also predates Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis's July 14 pitch for a FINRA-style, US-based Frontier AI Standards Body — the seed the current talks reportedly grew from. A draft White House executive order covering similar ground was reportedly prepared and then stalled amid internal disagreement.

The three labs are not fully aligned on what the body should be. Anthropic leans toward eventual government partnership; OpenAI favors voluntary industry standards while separately backing select state-level bills; Google has said the least publicly. What they agree on is timing — Hassabis has floated standing up an organization before the end of 2026, which would make this one of the fastest pivots from rivalry to shared governance the industry has attempted.

02 · CORPORATE
Microsoft · MAI Governance

Nadella Joins the Pacing Camp, Opens Microsoft's AI Rulebook for Public Comment

Responding directly to Amodei's essay, Microsoft's CEO endorsed "deliberate pacing" and said the company will publish a Code of Conduct for its own MAI models today — the first time Microsoft has put its model-behavior standards into a public document.

"Any pursuit of superintelligence has to be grounded in the core principle that if the AI we build is not helping humanity and under human control, it's not worth pursuing," Satya Nadella wrote on X Saturday, welcoming the "deliberate pacing needed to get alignment right" alongside ideas like embedded, employee-level evaluators inside labs.

The Code of Conduct, covering Microsoft's first-party MAI lineup — MAI-Thinking-1, the MAI-Image models, MAI-Voice and MAI-Transcribe — goes up today for open public consultation rather than arriving as a finished policy. Nadella framed governance as something that "cannot be controlled by a handful of entities" and must draw on academia and the wider ecosystem instead.

Mustafa Suleyman, who leads Microsoft AI, backed the move on X, calling human-serving technology "straightforward common sense" while adding "we're not yet at that point" on superintelligence risk. Nadella's statement lands two days after Sam Altman publicly agreed with Amodei's pacing essay — pulling a third major lab chief into the same posture within 72 hours.

03 · MARKETS
IPO Runway · Unit Economics

Anthropic Tells Investors It Just Booked a Second Straight Profitable Quarter

Ahead of a Nasdaq listing that could value it near $2 trillion, Anthropic disclosed Q2 revenue of $11.5 billion — up 14-fold year over year — and gross margins above 80%, a rare profitability claim from a frontier lab still spending heavily on compute.
80%+ Gross Margin

Anthropic recorded positive adjusted operating income for the second straight quarter, the Financial Times reported Sunday, citing people familiar with the figures. Revenue reached $11.5 billion in Q2, up from $787 million a year earlier, with annualized revenue hitting $65 billion at the end of July — up from $9 billion at the end of 2025.

The 80%-plus gross margin figure is calculated before revenue shared with distribution partners such as Amazon and before the cost of training its models — meaning the headline number flatters the picture somewhat, but still places Anthropic closer to a mature software company's margin profile than a capital-heavy infrastructure business.

The disclosure lands as Anthropic finalizes IPO logistics on a compressed, politically sensitive calendar: a Nasdaq listing, a roadshow now expected in mid-October, and underwriters led by Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan. Sustained profitability, if it holds up under public-market scrutiny, would be the single strongest card Anthropic can show skeptical investors before pricing what could be one of the largest tech listings on record.

04 · POLITICS
Wall Street · The White House

The Slowdown Pledge Just Met the Two Forces It Wasn't Built to Survive

Bloomberg's read on the pacing consensus: Anthropic and OpenAI now have to weigh their own braking calls against a tech industry, a financial market and a Trump administration that are all "likely to resist any substantial slowdown."

Published Monday, the Bloomberg piece frames the weekend's cascade of pacing statements — Amodei's essay, Altman's agreement, Nadella's Code of Conduct — as running straight into structural resistance rather than a receptive market. The measures, the report notes, explicitly followed a series of security breaches, not a change of heart about the race itself.

That resistance has a face: President Trump, who on Saturday brushed off extinction-risk warnings entirely, telling reporters his only concern is "if we don't win AI, we're going to be put in a very bad position." Wall Street's incentives point the same direction — a $2 trillion Anthropic listing and a market pricing continued acceleration are not naturally compatible with voluntary braking.

The tension sets up a genuinely open question for the rest of 2026: whether pacing becomes an enforceable institution — the standards body reported in today's lead story is the labs' own hedge against exactly this — or stays a set of essays and social posts that the market and the White House simply route around.

05 · RESEARCH
Coding Agents · Enterprise Benchmarks

A New Benchmark Says the Best Coding Agent Isn't the Cheapest One

Real-SWE, a fresh benchmark built on licensed private enterprise codebases, has Claude Fable 5.1 winning on resolution rate — and losing badly on cost per fix, next to Gemini 3.8 Flash and GPT-6 Astra.
38.8% Fable 5.1 Resolution

Y Combinator-backed Specific Labs built Real-SWE from private, licensed production codebases — billing, tax and customer-migration work spanning a median of 11 files per task — specifically to avoid the memorization problems that plague public leaderboards like SWE-bench. Each agent runs pass@1 across eight runs per task in an isolated sandbox.

Claude Fable 5.1 topped resolution at 38.8%, ahead of GPT-6 Astra's 33.8% and Gemini 3.8 Flash's 31.2%. But on cost, the ranking flips: Fable 5.1 runs about $6.96 per rollout, working out to roughly $17.94 per resolved task, while Gemini 3.8 Flash resolves a task for about $8.01 despite its lower hit rate.

The benchmark's own framing is the more useful takeaway than either leaderboard slot: to land ten resolved tasks, Fable needs about 25.8 attempts against Flash's 32.1 — meaning Flash saves roughly $99 in model spend but hands reviewers about six more failed attempts to catch. Which model is "better" now depends entirely on how expensive a missed bad fix is to your own review pipeline.
